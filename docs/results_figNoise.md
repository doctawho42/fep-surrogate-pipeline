# Fig Noise: the label-noise floor under the visible fraction

Generated by `figs/make_figNoise.py` (`make figNoise`). The constants, the three-level
noise ladder, the shrinkage grid, the draw count and the exactness tolerance are fixed in
that script's docstring and were written before any number below was computed. Nothing here
edits an existing results record; this document only adds.

The quantity under test is the visible (auditable) fraction
`f = ||Pi eps_tilde||^2 / ||eps_tilde||^2`, with `eps_e` the per-edge error against
experiment, `eps_tilde = eps / se_e`, and `Pi` the residual projector of the whitened
incidence matrix, measured on the same four grounded systems Fig Hodge uses.

## Headline: the crossing points

Pooled `f` = 0.0060. It would first reach its pooled chance level at a
denominator shrinkage of **50.6x** in the whitened metric and
**14.1x** in the isotropic one. The worst single system is
`bace`: 4.2x whitened, 2.0x isotropic, and
1.36x isotropic under the least favourable single-edge deletion.

Against that, the shrinkage label noise can actually supply, pooled:
* **L1** (`sigma_edge` = 1.31 kcal/mol): 14.13x pooled, 4.13x with
  each system capped at its own measured error; impossible on 3 of 4 systems.
* **L2** (`sigma_edge` = 0.93 kcal/mol): 1.87x pooled, 1.87x with
  each system capped at its own measured error; impossible on 0 of 4 systems.
* **L3** (`sigma_edge` = 0.66 kcal/mol): 1.30x pooled, 1.30x with
  each system capped at its own measured error; impossible on 0 of 4 systems.

So the bound is comfortable in the whitened metric -- the metric `f` is defined in -- and it
is NOT comfortable in the isotropic one. The readings that go against the article's thesis
are stated first; there are 6 of them, all on `p38` and `bace`, and all but one under
the isotropic convention:

* `bace`, isotropic: needs 2.00x, L2 supplies 2.55x -- the margin is erased.
* `bace`, isotropic, worst deletion: needs 1.36x, L2 supplies 2.55x -- the margin is erased.
* `bace`, whitened, worst deletion: needs 2.55x, L2 supplies 2.55x -- the margin is erased (a tie, within 1 per cent).
* `bace`, isotropic, worst deletion: needs 1.36x, L3 supplies 1.44x -- the margin is erased.
* `p38`, isotropic, worst deletion: needs 1.46x, L2 supplies 9.22x -- the margin is erased.
* `p38`, isotropic, worst deletion: needs 1.46x, L3 supplies 1.80x -- the margin is erased.

L1, the only level with a literature measurement behind it, is refuted outright on 3 of
the 4 systems: it asserts more label variance than the measured error contains,
so it is counted neither as erasing a margin nor as leaving one intact. See N3.

And a near-coincidence worth stating so it is not mistaken for an identity: the pooled
shrinkage L1 supplies without the per-system cap, 14.13x, is
within one per cent of the 14.08x needed to reach the pooled
isotropic chance level. They are different quantities that happen to land together here; L1
is in any case impossible on three of the four systems taken singly.

## N1 Label noise is annihilated, numerically

A synthetic per-ligand error `b ~ N(0, sigma^2)` with `sigma = 0.9277` kcal/mol
(0.68 log10 at 1.3642 kcal/mol per log10, the L1 level) is added to each network as the
gradient field `eps_e -> eps_e + b_j - b_i`, 200 seeded draws per system. Both the numerator
and the denominator of `f` are recomputed on the perturbed field.

| system | numerator ||Pi eps~||^2 | worst change, rel. to numerator | rel. to denominator | denominator ||eps~||^2 | median growth | growth range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `cdk8` | 14.8791 | 1.96e-11 | 1.27e-13 | 2294.34 | 1.91x | [0.38x, 8.66x] |
| `hif2a` | 68.5251 | 1.76e-11 | 3.03e-14 | 39841.9 | 1.56x | [0.54x, 5.02x] |
| `p38` | 91.6505 | 1.54e-11 | 1.32e-13 | 10637.9 | 2.24x | [0.35x, 8.48x] |
| `bace` | 165.324 | 6.77e-14 | 3.12e-15 | 3594.76 | 2.16x | [0.85x, 5.55x] |

Worst relative change in the numerator over all systems and all 800 draws:
1.96e-11, against the pre-registered tolerance of 1e-10. The exactness claim
holds. That residue is floating-point conditioning in the pseudo-inverse, not a real
signal: measured against the injected field's own scale, the denominator, it is at most
1.3e-13. The denominator meanwhile grows by
1.6x to 2.2x
at the median (individual draws can shrink it, since an injected field can partly cancel the
measured one; the median over 200 draws is the summary).

Label noise is invisible to the numerator by construction, so any correction for it can only
move `f` UPWARD. The measured `f` is a LOWER bound on the share of the error attributable to
force-field bias, and everything below asks how far up the correction could push it.

## N2 Denominator shrinkage, and where the bound would first be lost

If a share `r` of the measured per-edge error variance is label noise, the denominator
shrinks by `s = 1/(1-r)` and `f` rises to `s*f` exactly, since the numerator does not move.
Both chance levels are the ones the article already reports: `dof/E` in the whitened metric,
and `tr(Pi W)/tr(W)` for an error isotropic in kcal/mol.

## N3 What shrinkage is actually available

A per-edge label noise of standard deviation `sigma_edge` contributes an expected whitened
squared norm `sigma_edge^2 * sum_e 1/se_e^2`. Its share `r` of the measured `||eps_tilde||^2`
is the shrinkage's only input. `r >= 1` means the level claims more label noise than the
network's whole measured error against experiment contains: arithmetically impossible,
reported as such rather than clipped.

The three levels are a ladder in per-edge label-noise variance. L1 is the measured literature
quantity; L2 and L3 are stated reductions of it, not independent measurements. Kalliokoski
et al. measured the MIXED-laboratory regime, whereas the article's grounding uses one assay
per system, IC50 before Ki, never mixed, so L1 is an upper bound and not an estimate.

* **L1**: `sigma_edge` = 1.3119 kcal/mol -- per-ligand independent; full Kalliokoski variance.
* **L2**: `sigma_edge` = 0.9277 kcal/mol -- half of L1's variance; shared assay term cancels in the difference.
* **L3**: `sigma_edge` = 0.6560 kcal/mol -- a quarter of L1's variance; tighter single-assay regime.

**L1, the only level with a literature measurement behind it, is arithmetically impossible on
three of the four systems.** On `cdk8`, `p38` and `bace` a mixed-laboratory per-ligand sigma of
0.68 log10 would put more label noise into the network than its entire measured error against
experiment contains, whitened. That is the direct evidence that 0.68 log10 is an upper bound
rather than an estimate for these curated single-assay groundings, and it is a fact about the
data rather than an assumption imported to protect the bound.

The pooled row is a ratio of sums, so it can carry a level that no single system can. Both
pooled figures are therefore reported: the plain one, and one that caps each system's label
variance at its own measured total. The capped figure is the SMALLER and hence the one
favourable to the article, so it is never quoted alone.

The whitened column is the one that governs `f`, since `f` lives in the whitened metric. The
unweighted column is reported beside it because it is what a reader computes from the
article's quoted per-edge error in kcal/mol, and the two do not agree: whitening weights an
edge by `1/se^2`, and the grounded networks' reported standard errors span 0.14 to 0.40

Measured per-edge error: `cdk8` rms 1.86, median |eps| 1.45, median se 0.40, `hif2a` rms 1.99, median |eps| 1.22, median se 0.23, `p38` rms 1.65, median |eps| 1.06, median se 0.36, `bace` rms 1.55, median |eps| 1.15, median se 0.14 kcal/mol.

## What this does and does not license

* The correction is one-directional: `f` can only rise. The article's 0.6% is a floor.
* In the whitened metric the pooled bound needs a 50.6x shrinkage to be lost and the most
  any level supplies pooled is 4.13x once each system is held to its own arithmetic. The
  bound survives the correction there by an order of magnitude.
* In the isotropic metric it does not survive uniformly. `bace` under a single-edge deletion
  needs only 1.36x, which even L3 -- a quarter of the mixed-laboratory variance -- supplies.
  `p38` under a single-edge deletion needs 1.46x and L3 supplies 1.80x. The isotropic reading
  of the bound is therefore not robust to a label-noise correction on the two systems where
  it was already weakest, and the revision should not claim that it is.
* Nothing here measures the label noise in these particular ChEMBL series. It bounds what a
  literature-scale label noise could do. A direct measurement would need replicate
  assay values per ligand, which the grounding sources do not carry.
