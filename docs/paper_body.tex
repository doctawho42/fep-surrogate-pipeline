\begin{abstract}
The aleatoric (finite-sampling) uncertainty of a relative binding free energy (RBFE)
estimate need not be learned: it can be read off the physics in closed, differentiable form.
Placing a differentiable Bennett Acceptance Ratio (BAR) estimator at a surrogate's bottleneck
yields the per-edge \emph{sandwich} variance $B/I^2$ with an $O(1)$ backward pass; the readout
reproduces \texttt{pymbar}'s MBAR uncertainty and Monte-Carlo truth across overlap regimes
with no Monte-Carlo, whereas a learned-variance head cannot even identify a per-edge variance
from one label per edge and is $5$--$12\times$ overconfident. Because it is a closed form, the
variance is differentiable into a Fisher--resistance graph Laplacian (whose acquisition is
provably zero on gauge-redundant directions) and exposes the one parity-odd readout channel an
equivariant trunk needs to resolve enantiomers ($11.7\times$ lower chiral $\ddG$ error). We
then ask, and answer honestly, a question most ML-for-FEP papers skip: does this free,
calibrated uncertainty actually improve downstream machine learning? Across the public OpenFF
protein--ligand benchmark and controlled simulations, we find that it largely does not. On
real out-of-distribution residuals the decomposed interval is \emph{not} sharper than a flat
conformal band; at matched decision budget the choice of $\sigma$ does not improve which edges
are selected or which molecules are committed to synthesis; and steering a generator by a
calibrated reward does not help---on a $\sigma$-varying target it actively hurts, because the
uncertainty tracks chemical novelty, not low quality. What calibration \emph{does} buy is
trust: an overconfident $\sigma$ silently corrupts stop/commit decisions, while any properly
calibrated $\sigma$---including the physics one, at no calibration-set cost---makes them
reliable. We scope these claims to the regimes tested (a single decomposed $\sigma$, one public
benchmark, controlled simulations; the real-binding calibration is an internal bootstrap check
pending independent-replicate validation), and we state what would overturn them: where the
uncertainty tracks \emph{quality} rather than chemical novelty---diverse-library lead
optimization rather than congeneric de-novo design---a calibrated reward should help generation,
exactly as the measured $\mathrm{corr}(\sigma,\text{affinity})=+0.42$ predicts. We prove four
results and verify every constant numerically; in these regimes, calibration acts as a decision
instrument rather than a performance multiplier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Relative binding free energy calculations are the accuracy standard of structure-based drug
design, but each edge of a perturbation network costs hours of molecular dynamics. Machine
learning surrogates promise to triage which edges to run, and increasingly the bottleneck is
not the point prediction but the \emph{uncertainty}: active learning, network design, and
stop/go decisions all consume a per-edge error bar, and a miscalibrated bar silently corrupts
every downstream decision. The dominant recipe estimates that uncertainty by \emph{learning}
it---a mean--variance head~\citep{nix1994mve}, a deep ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles},
or a conformal wrapper~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}. We argue this discards information the
physics already contains. The free-energy estimator at the end of every FEP edge---the Bennett
Acceptance Ratio (BAR)~\citep{bennett1976bar}---is an $M$-estimator whose finite-sampling
variance has a known closed form. Our central design choice is to make that estimator the
bottleneck of the surrogate and to read its variance off analytically rather than learn it.
This is one instance of a single principle that runs through the paper---\emph{compute what the
physics or geometry already determines, and learn only what they do not}: the sampling variance
is read off the BAR estimator, the chirality bit off a geometric pseudoscalar, and the
identifiable directions off the measurement graph. We then audit, honestly, whether such a
computed-for-free uncertainty actually improves downstream learning---and find that its value is
trust in a decision, not a boost in sharpness, ranking, or generation.

\paragraph{What is, and is not, new.} It is well understood in the free-energy community that
the naive inverse-Fisher-information $1/I$ over-states the BAR variance, and the field-standard
tool \texttt{pymbar} already reports the corrected MBAR uncertainty~\citep{shirts2008mbar}; we
do \emph{not} claim to fix FEP variance. Our contributions are four. (1) The BAR sandwich
variance as a \emph{differentiable closed form} with an $O(1)$ backward, so it propagates into
a surrogate and into an acquisition graph---\texttt{pymbar} returns a number, not a
back-propagatable graph weight. (2) A Fisher--resistance correspondence that turns the per-edge
variances into a graph Laplacian and yields a gauge-aware, cost-aware acquisition. (3) A
chirality-completeness statement identifying the one readout channel an equivariant trunk must
contain. (4) Crucially, an \emph{honest audit} of whether this free, calibrated uncertainty
improves downstream learning. We report---on real benchmark data and controlled
simulations---that it mostly does not: it is no sharper than flat conformal on real
out-of-distribution residuals, does not improve ranking or commit selection at matched budget,
and does not improve (and on a $\sigma$-varying target hurts) a generator. Calibration buys
\emph{trust} in stop/commit decisions, not performance. We pair every claim with a falsifiable
figure and report the experiments where the method does not beat a simple baseline.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Free-energy estimators.} BAR~\citep{bennett1976bar} and its multistate
generalization MBAR~\citep{shirts2008mbar} are the standard estimators for alchemical free
energies; FEP+~\citep{wang2015fep} brought them to prospective potency prediction, and large
retrospective assessments~\citep{schindler2020merck} and best-practice guides~\citep{mey2020alchem}
document both their accuracy and their per-edge uncertainty. Our contribution is not a new
estimator but the observation that this uncertainty is a differentiable closed form---the
sandwich variance of $M$-estimation~\citep{huber1967,white1982sandwich}---readable off the
bottleneck and back-propagatable.

\paragraph{Learned and recalibrated uncertainty.} The prevailing alternative is to learn
predictive uncertainty: a mean--variance head by Gaussian NLL~\citep{nix1994mve}, a deep
ensemble for the epistemic term~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, the aleatoric/epistemic
split~\citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, or distribution-free conformal
prediction~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}, including quantile-adaptive variants such as
CQR~\citep{romano2019cqr}. A predicted $\sigma$ can also be \emph{recalibrated} post hoc by
temperature/Platt scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or isotonic
regression~\citep{zadrozny2002isotonic}. We use these as baselines: the honest question is not
whether the physics $\sigma$ beats a \emph{raw} overconfident head, but whether it beats a
\emph{properly recalibrated} one---and on which downstream task.

\paragraph{Equivariant trunks and chirality.} $E(3)$-equivariant
networks~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} are the de-facto trunks for
3D molecular property prediction, and the use of parity-\emph{odd} (pseudoscalar) irreps to
break achirality is standard in that literature; we make the architectural requirement precise
and verify it.

\paragraph{Acquisition and generation.} Acquisition by the knowledge gradient~\citep{frazier2008kg}
and its Monte-Carlo implementations~\citep{balandat2020botorch,gardner2018gpytorch} drive edge
selection, while GFlowNets~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} sample candidates in
proportion to reward. We connect the per-edge sandwich variances to a Fisher--resistance graph
Laplacian (whose contrast variance is the resistance distance~\citep{klein1993resistance}) for
gauge-aware acquisition, and audit calibration's effect on selection and generation on the
OpenFF protein--ligand benchmark~\citep{hahn2022plbench}.

\section{The BAR information bottleneck}\label{sec:bar}
\paragraph{Setup.} For a single alchemical edge, forward and reverse works
$\{W^f_i\}_{i=1}^{n_f}$, $\{W^r_j\}_{j=1}^{n_r}$ define unified coordinates $x_i=W^f_i$,
$x_j=-W^r_j$. With $M=\ln(n_f/n_r)$ and $p(x;d)=\sigma(x-d+M)$, the BAR estimate $\dFh$ is the
root of the score $S(d)=\sum_{j\in r}p(x_j)-\sum_{i\in f}(1-p(x_i))$, and
$\partial_d S=-\sum p(1-p)=:-I(d)$, the curvature/overlap.

\begin{theorem}[$O(1)$ differentiable backward]\label{thm:back}
Let $\dFh(\theta)$ be defined by $S(\dFh,\theta)=0$ with non-degenerate curvature $I\neq0$
(positive overlap). The implicit function theorem gives $d\dFh/d\theta=I^{-1}\partial_\theta S$.
For window means, $d\dFh/d\mu^f=I^f/I$, $d\dFh/d\mu^r=-I^r/I$, with $|I^f/I|+|I^r/I|=1$. The
backward pass is one division by the curvature; the root-find is not unrolled.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sampling variance via the sandwich]\label{thm:sand}
$\dFh$ is an $M$-estimator; its asymptotic sampling variance is the sandwich
$\Var(\dFh)=A^{-1}BA^{-1}=B/I^2$, with $A=I$ and $B=n_f\Var_f[p]+n_r\Var_r[p]$
\citep{huber1967}. Here $A\neq B$ arises from BAR's \emph{stratified two-sample design}, not
from model misspecification; the sandwich is the sample-computable form and equals the
Bennett/Shirts--Chodera (MBAR) uncertainty to leading order in $1/n$
\citep{bennett1976bar,shirts2008mbar}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[scope of ``calibrated'']
$B/I^2$ is the variance of the \emph{sampling} error of $\dFh$, conditional on the works being
drawn from the correct alchemical ensembles. It says nothing about systematic force-field bias,
which is outside $B/I^2$ (we attach a separate epistemic/correction term in
Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The value of Theorem~\ref{thm:sand} for us is that this term is a
differentiable closed form (Theorem~\ref{thm:back}) and is non-negative by construction, where
the plug-in MBAR expression can return a negative value at near-zero overlap.
\end{remark}

The information equality $A=B$ (which would give $1/I$) holds for a single-sample MLE but fails
for BAR's two-sample design; we verify the gradients to machine precision and the variance to
$\le$1\% against $2000$-replicate Monte-Carlo and \texttt{pymbar} in the good-overlap regime
(at extreme low overlap, $n{=}20$, the ddof-corrected plug-in runs $\sim$10\% below MBAR, both
$\approx1$).

\paragraph{Fig.~\ref{fig:A}: the readout is correct; the learned foil cannot be.}
Figure~\ref{fig:A}A sweeps the work-distribution overlap. The sandwich $B/I^2$ coincides with
MBAR and Monte-Carlo truth across the range (reported/true se $\approx1$), with no Monte-Carlo.
A learned mean--variance head trained by Gaussian NLL on a realistic budget of $\sim$200 edges
with \emph{one} noisy $\ddG$ label each cannot identify a per-edge sampling variance from a
single realization---at best, given the right inputs (e.g.\ overlap features) and enough data, a
learned head \emph{approximates} the closed form that the bottleneck computes exactly, per edge,
and for free---and here it is $5$--$12\times$ overconfident (reported se $0.08$--$0.18\times$ the
truth, regime-dependent). Bare $1/I$ is shown only as the
information-equality plug-in it corrects ($1.08$--$2.65\times$, worst at high overlap), not as a
baseline anyone reports. Figure~\ref{fig:A}B checks calibration on real protein--ligand BACE1
RBFE binding edges (19 edges; sandwich/bootstrap-truth $1.00$, $[0.96,1.07]$); we note this
bootstrap reference reuses the same work samples and is therefore an internal consistency check,
not an independent-replicate validation (a limitation, not a headline).

\section{From a variance to a graph: gauge-aware acquisition}\label{sec:gauge}
\begin{theorem}[Fisher--resistance correspondence]\label{thm:fr}
For relative measurements $y_e=(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)+\varepsilon_e$,
$\varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,V_e)$ with $V_e=B_e/I_e^2$, the Gaussian posterior precision of
the node potentials is the weighted Laplacian $L=\sum_e w_e(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^\top$ with
conductances $w_e=1/V_e=I_e^2/B_e$. Contrast variance equals effective
resistance~\citep{klein1993resistance}, a single edge recovers $V_e$, Sherman--Morrison gives
$O(1)$ acquisition updates, and $\ker L\supseteq\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$, so the knowledge
gradient~\citep{frazier2008kg} is exactly zero on gauge/redundant directions: the acquisition is
automatically gauge-invariant.
\end{theorem}
The edge weights are the inverse sandwich variances---the differentiable readout of
Section~\ref{sec:bar} entering the decision layer, which a black-box uncertainty number cannot
provide. Figure~\ref{fig:D} shows the knowledge gradient on the all-ones gauge contrast is
$10^{-21}$ (exact zero, an identifiability statement that transfers structurally to multi-target
networks), and that a gauge-\emph{unaware} acquisition wastes budget resolving a
decision-irrelevant cross-cluster offset, reaching the within-target top-$k$ at regret $0.27$
where the gauge-aware one reaches $0.01$. This experiment is a controlled simulation.

the gauge (all-ones) contrast and routes value to decision-relevant edges. (B) Ablating
gauge-awareness wastes budget on redundant cross-cluster bridges.}
\label{fig:D}
\end{figure}

\section{Chirality completeness}\label{sec:chiral}
An equivariant trunk~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} feeds the
bottleneck. Which readout channels does it need to distinguish stereoisomers?
\begin{theorem}[Chirality completeness]\label{thm:chiral}
Any $O(3)$-invariant readout (a function of pairwise distances, norms, or dot products) is
constant on an enantiomer pair $\{M,\sigma M\}$ and is therefore enantiomer-blind, so
\emph{some} parity-odd channel is necessary. The pseudoscalar
$\chi(M)=\det[p_2{-}p_1,p_3{-}p_1,p_4{-}p_1]$ (the $\texttt{0o}$ triple product) satisfies
$\chi(AM)=\det(A)\chi(M)$, so it is $SO(3)$-invariant and flips sign under reflection;
$(\{\|p_i{-}p_j\|\},\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\chi)$ is a complete $SO(3)$ invariant. Thus a single
$\texttt{0o}$ triple-product channel is a \emph{sufficient} minimal addition that restores the
chirality bit.
\end{theorem}
Consistent with the paper's principle, the chirality bit is \emph{read off} the geometry (a
pseudoscalar), not learned; this makes precise a fact familiar from the equivariant-network
literature (the role of odd irreps), stated here as an architectural-completeness requirement on
the trunk that feeds the bottleneck rather than as a central result. Figure~\ref{fig:E} realizes
it: an even readout gives bit-identical outputs on every
enantiomer pair (collapse $=0$ to machine precision), so its best possible chiral $\ddG$
prediction is zero; adding the $\texttt{0o}$ channel cuts enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE $11.7\times$
($1.67\to0.14$ kcal/mol).

\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figE_chirality_completeness.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A parity-odd channel is necessary and one triple product suffices.}
(A) The even ($O(3)$) readout collapses enantiomers exactly; $\texttt{0o}$ separates them.
(B) Enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE drops $11.7\times$ when the $\texttt{0o}$ channel is added.}

\section{An honest audit: where calibration helps downstream---and where it does not}\label{sec:audit}
The free, differentiable $\sigma$ is attractive; the operative question is whether it improves
downstream machine learning. We test it on four tasks---interval sharpness, edge ranking,
commit-to-synthesis selection, and target-directed generation---on the public OpenFF
protein--ligand benchmark~\citep{hahn2022plbench}, which aggregates the FEP+ and Merck data
sets~\citep{wang2015fep,schindler2020merck} (experimental $\ddG$ labels; both-endpoint
scaffold-disjoint out-of-distribution splits), and in controlled simulations. The amortized
$\sigma$ we test is a deep-ensemble trunk's decomposed
$\sigma_{\text{total}}=q\sqrt{\sigma^2_{\text{ens}}+\sigma^2_{\text{ale}}}$ (epistemic ensemble
spread, a benchmark aleatoric floor, conformally scaled by $q$).

\paragraph{Trust: an overconfident $\sigma$ corrupts decisions; a calibrated one does not---and
calibration is cheap.} The clearest payoff of calibration is a trustworthy stop/commit
decision. Figure~\ref{fig:J}B isolates the effect of $\sigma$ by holding the (ensemble) mean
fixed and varying only the uncertainty used to commit a candidate to synthesis (commit iff the
lower-confidence bound $\mu-z_{1-\alpha}\sigma\ge\tau$). An overconfident epistemic-only
$\sigma$ over-claims badly---its realized-versus-claimed correctness shortfall is $+0.182$
(CI $[0.132,0.225]$) larger than the decomposed $\sigma$'s---whereas the decomposed physics
$\sigma$ is calibrated-to-conservative. Importantly, this trust is \emph{not unique} to
the physics $\sigma$: applying standard temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or
split-conformal recalibration to the same overconfident $\sigma$ recovers the trustworthiness
(temperature$-$ours $+0.034$, CI $[-0.018,0.081]$; conformal$-$ours $-0.029$, CI
$[-0.077,0.007]$; both indistinguishable from ours). The honest reading is that trustworthy
decisions require \emph{some} proper calibration, which the physics $\sigma$ supplies at no
calibration-set cost for the aleatoric term and, uniquely, as a differentiable graph weight.

\paragraph{Sharpness: not sharper on real out-of-distribution residuals.} A natural hope is
that a decomposed, adaptive $\sigma$ yields tighter intervals than a flat conformal band at
equal coverage. On a controlled 1-D problem whose generative structure the decomposition knows,
it does (we keep that illustration in the supplement). On \emph{real} held-out trunk residuals
(Fig.~\ref{fig:Breal}), at matched $\sim$0.90 marginal coverage, it does \emph{not}: the
decomposed interval is slightly \emph{wider} than flat split-conformal (width ratio
split/ours $0.92\times$), because the epistemic spread is small relative to the large
irreducible OOD error (correlation of half-width with $|$residual$|$ only $+0.11$). Its
conditional calibration is marginally better than a flat band and comparable to a fair
Mondrian-by-target conformal (per-target max coverage error $0.17$ vs $0.22$ for split and
$0.22$ for Mondrian), but the advantage is not significant
(ours$-$split $-0.044$, CI $[-0.121,0.005]$). The synthetic ``$2\times$ sharper'' result does
not transfer to real data.

\paragraph{Ranking and selection: the choice of $\sigma$ is second-order at matched budget.}
For edge ranking in active learning, sandwich versus naive edge weights differ by
$\le$$1.05\times$ across calls-, A-optimal-, and cost-to-top-$k$ metrics
(Fig.~\ref{fig:C}, supplement): in a connected measurement graph the GLS contrast mean is
unbiased for \emph{any} positive weights (Gauss--Markov), so the weight choice is second-order
for ranking. The same holds for commit selection: at matched commit \emph{volume}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:J}A, ranking candidates by the standardized margin $(\mu-\tau)/\sigma$ and
committing the top fraction), the $\sigma$-aware ranking does not beat a raw-$\mu$ ranking
(precision at a 25\% budget $0.674$ vs $0.680$; trunk$-$raw $-0.006$, CI $[-0.016,0.007]$). A
calibrated bar tells you \emph{whether} to act; it does not change \emph{which} action ranks
first.

\paragraph{Generation: calibration does not help, and where $\sigma$ varies it hurts.} We drive
a trajectory-balance GFlowNet~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} over an evaluable ligand
space (a held-out target's measured congeneric series; trunk trained target-disjoint) with three
rewards: the calibrated lower-confidence bound, the raw predicted value, and an overconfident
MVE reward. On \texttt{eg5}, the on-disk target with the \emph{largest} $\sigma$ spread (so the
calibrated reward genuinely re-ranks candidates), the calibrated reward \emph{lowers} the true
hit-rate of the generated distribution ($0.140$ vs raw $0.250$ and MVE $0.256$; cal$-$raw
$-0.110$, CI $[-0.151,-0.073]$; Fig.~\ref{fig:K}). The mechanism is instructive:
$\mathrm{corr}(\sigma_{\text{total}},\text{true affinity})=+0.42$---the high-$\sigma$ ligands are
the chemically novel \emph{strong} binders, so the LCB penalty $-\kappa\sigma$ demotes the true
winners. Calibration would help generation only if $\sigma$ flagged \emph{low-quality}
candidates; on congeneric chemistry it flags \emph{novelty} instead. This is the steelman target
for calibration-in-generation, and it fails. More usefully, it yields a transferable diagnostic
for calibrated active generation: a lower-confidence-bound reward helps only when $\sigma$ flags
\emph{low quality}; whenever $\sigma$ instead flags \emph{novelty}---the common case on
congeneric or lead-like series---the bound penalizes exactly the candidates worth making, and an
uncalibrated reward is the safer default. A regime where $\sigma$ tracks quality (e.g.\ diverse
libraries with genuine actives and decoys) is where calibrated generation should pay, and is the
natural next test.

\paragraph{Where it pays: the stopping decision.} The one place a calibrated bar clearly pays is
knowing when to \emph{stop} an active-learning loop. Figure~\ref{fig:G} runs two learners with
the same correct posterior mean but different assumed uncertainty: the calibrated learner's
claimed confidence tracks actual top-$k$ correctness ($|\Delta|=0.022$) and stops at $30$ calls
$82\%$ correct, while an overconfident learner (using the learned head's $0.15\times$ scale from
Fig.~\ref{fig:A}) stops twice as early at only $60\%$ correct. This is a controlled simulation,
but it is the same lesson as the commit panel: calibration is a trust instrument.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} Five independent tests---sharpness, edge weighting, commit ranking,
generation, and (positively) stopping---paint one coherent picture. A calibrated per-edge
$\sigma$ does not sharpen intervals on real data, does not re-rank what to compute or commit,
and does not steer a generator toward better molecules; an \emph{overconfident} $\sigma$,
however, silently corrupts the stop/commit decision. Calibration is therefore a
\emph{trust/decision instrument}, not a performance multiplier---and the physics bottleneck
delivers that calibration for free and differentiably. The differentiable graph weight earns its
place through \emph{structure}, not magnitude: it makes the gauge directions exactly
unidentifiable (knowledge gradient $=0$) and supplies the trust calculation, even though---by the
same Gauss--Markov argument that makes weighting second-order---the numerical \emph{value} of the
weight does not improve ranking. The value of reading the variance off the physics is therefore a
calibrated decision and an identifiable acquisition, not a sharper or better-ranked one.

\section{Limitations}
The sandwich is aleatoric-only and conditional on correctly sampled ensembles; systematic
force-field bias is outside it, and the out-of-distribution behavior we audit rests on an
ensemble plus a scalar aleatoric floor (there is no learned force-field-correction head, so the
three-way decomposition of the synthetic illustration is not reproduced on real data). The
binding-edge calibration (Fig.~\ref{fig:A}B) uses a bootstrap of the same works rather than
independent replicates; an OpenFE-style three-replicate validation is the natural next step. The
gauge-acquisition and stopping experiments are controlled simulations. The generation audit is a
single $\sigma$-varying target; the conclusion that $\sigma$ tracks novelty rather than quality
should be tested across more chemotypes. Finally, a structure-based (docking) reverse-screen we
built as a follow-on did not beat a ligand-similarity baseline on this benchmark, so the
structure-directed regime is left to future work rather than claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}
A differentiable BAR estimator at a surrogate's bottleneck makes the aleatoric uncertainty a
computed, differentiable, non-negative closed form rather than a learned guess, usable as a
Fisher--resistance graph weight (whose value is structural---gauge-identifiability and
trust---rather than a better-ranking magnitude) and paired with an exact chirality requirement.
Tested honestly, this free calibration does not improve downstream sharpness, ranking, or
generation; what it buys---and what an overconfident learned $\sigma$ destroys---is trustworthy
stop/commit decisions. Four theorems and a suite of falsifiable figures, including several
pre-registered negatives, delimit what the construction does and does not buy: in the regimes we
tested, calibration is a decision instrument rather than a performance multiplier; where the
uncertainty would track quality rather than novelty, that verdict should be revisited.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every figure regenerates from one command; all theorem constants
are unit tests; all data are public (alchemtest BACE1/benzene, the OpenFF protein--ligand
benchmark, ChEMBL). Recalibration baselines use temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration}
and isotonic regression~\citep{zadrozny2002isotonic}.
