\begin{abstract}
We turn a routine consistency check into a calibrated diagnostic for relative binding free energy
(RBFE). At a surrogate's bottleneck, the Bennett Acceptance Ratio (BAR) \emph{sandwich} variance
$B/I^2$ is a closed, non-negative, $O(1)$-differentiable aleatoric-variance layer that reads each
alchemical edge's finite-sampling uncertainty off the physics (\texttt{pymbar} returns a number, not
a back-propagatable graph weight). Its decisive payoff is \emph{detection}: the calibrated null that
turns thermodynamic cycle-closure into a quality-control test, flagging six systematic-error networks
(of $48$) on $1145$ real OpenFE binding edges with distribution-free causal and cross-replicate
backstops, where an overconfident learned $\sigma$ flags $88\%$ of systems and is useless. That bar
is trustworthy for the job: on the same $1145$ edges the MBAR form (equal to the sandwich to leading
order) is conservative by $1.41\times$ ($1.25\times$ bias-corrected), never systematically
overconfident. The same free primitive supplies a Fisher--resistance acquisition-graph weight (the
knowledge gradient is exactly zero on gauge directions) and exposes a parity-odd (\texttt{0o}) readout
channel that resolves enantiomers ($11.7\times$ lower chiral $\ddG$ error). We then map, honestly,
where this free calibration helps and where it does not: across public benchmarks and controlled
simulations it is no sharper than conformal on out-of-distribution residuals, does not change edge
selection or commit ranking, and does not help reward re-ranking on a measured congeneric set
(\texttt{eg5}), so it is a \emph{decision-and-quality-control instrument, not a performance
multiplier}. A fair learned-variance foil, fed work moments \emph{and} the overlap scalar $I$, stays
$\sim$$7\times$ overconfident; a corrected $\beta$-NLL head and a $5$-member deep ensemble do no
better at a realistic budget, a data-efficiency limit of learned per-edge variance rather than an
identifiability one (pooling confirms the variance is identifiable). We
establish four results: two new (the differentiable closed-form sandwich and its $O(1)$ backward)
and two that make precise or connect known results (the Fisher--resistance correspondence and
chirality completeness). Together they map where a free, differentiable primitive helps: a decision
and quality-control instrument, not a performance multiplier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Relative binding free energy calculations are the accuracy standard of structure-based drug
design, but each edge of a perturbation network costs hours of molecular dynamics. Machine
learning surrogates promise to triage which edges to run, and increasingly the bottleneck is
not the point prediction but the \emph{uncertainty}: active learning, network design, and
stop/go decisions all consume a per-edge error bar, and a miscalibrated bar silently corrupts
every downstream decision. The dominant recipe estimates that uncertainty by \emph{learning}
it: a mean--variance (MVE) head~\citep{nix1994mve}, a deep ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles},
or a conformal wrapper~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}. We argue this discards information the
physics already contains. The free-energy estimator at the end of every FEP edge, the Bennett
Acceptance Ratio (BAR)~\citep{bennett1976bar}, is an $M$-estimator whose finite-sampling
variance has a known closed form. Our central design choice is to make that estimator the
bottleneck of the surrogate and to read its variance off analytically rather than learn it.
This is one instance of a single principle that runs through the paper (\emph{compute what the
physics or geometry already determines, and learn only what they do not}): the sampling variance
is read off the BAR estimator, the chirality bit off a geometric pseudoscalar, and the
identifiable directions off the measurement graph. We then ask, honestly, whether this
computed-for-free uncertainty improves downstream learning, and find its value is not a boost in
sharpness, ranking, or reward re-ranking but a \emph{decision and quality-control} one:
decisively, a calibrated cycle-closure test that separates systematic from sampling error on real
perturbation networks, and, in simulation, trustworthy stop/commit decisions that an overconfident
$\sigma$ corrupts.

\paragraph{What is, and is not, new.} It is well understood in the free-energy community that
the naive inverse-Fisher-information $1/I$ over-states the BAR variance, and the field-standard
tool \texttt{pymbar} already reports the corrected MBAR uncertainty~\citep{shirts2008mbar}; we
do \emph{not} claim to fix FEP variance. Our contributions are one demonstrated capability and
three supporting results. \emph{(Capability)} A \emph{calibrated cycle-closure quality-control
test}: the replicate-validated sandwich variance is the per-edge null that turns thermodynamic
closure into a detector with per-edge-adaptive thresholds and distribution-free causal and
cross-replicate backstops, flagging six chemically-sensible systematic-error networks of $48$ on
$1145$ real edges, a capability an overconfident $\sigma$ destroys (Section~\ref{sec:qc}). Four
results support and delimit it: two new (the differentiable closed-form sandwich and its $O(1)$
backward) and two that make precise or connect known results (the Fisher--resistance correspondence
and the chirality-completeness requirement). (1, new) The BAR sandwich variance as a
\emph{differentiable closed form} with an $O(1)$ backward, so it propagates into a surrogate and
into an acquisition graph and gives the quality-control null its per-edge-adaptive form.
\texttt{pymbar} returns a number, not a back-propagatable graph weight. (2,
make-precise) A Fisher--resistance correspondence that turns the per-edge variances into a graph
Laplacian and yields a gauge-aware, cost-aware acquisition. (3, make-precise) A
chirality-completeness statement identifying a parity-odd readout channel an equivariant trunk
must contain. (4) Finally, an \emph{honest applicability map} of where this free, calibrated
uncertainty helps downstream learning, and where it does not. We report (on real benchmark data and
controlled simulations) that it mostly does not: it is no sharper than flat conformal on real
out-of-distribution residuals, does not improve ranking or commit selection at matched budget,
and does not improve (and on a $\sigma$-varying target hurts) reward re-ranking over a measured
congeneric set (single target \texttt{eg5}). Calibration buys \emph{trust and detection}, not a
sharper or better-ranked prediction. We pair every claim with a falsifiable figure and report the
experiments where the method does not beat a simple baseline.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Free-energy estimators.} BAR~\citep{bennett1976bar} and its multistate
generalization MBAR~\citep{shirts2008mbar} are the standard estimators for alchemical free
energies; FEP+~\citep{wang2015fep} brought them to prospective potency prediction, and large
retrospective assessments~\citep{schindler2020merck} and best-practice guides~\citep{mey2020alchem}
document both their accuracy and their per-edge uncertainty. Our contribution is not a new
estimator but the observation that this uncertainty is a differentiable closed form, the
sandwich variance of $M$-estimation~\citep{huber1967,white1982sandwich}, readable off the
bottleneck and back-propagatable.

\paragraph{Learned and recalibrated uncertainty.} The prevailing alternative is to learn
predictive uncertainty: a mean--variance head by Gaussian NLL~\citep{nix1994mve}, a deep
ensemble for the epistemic term~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, the aleatoric/epistemic
split~\citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, or distribution-free conformal
prediction~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}, including quantile-adaptive variants such as
CQR~\citep{romano2019cqr}. A predicted $\sigma$ can also be \emph{recalibrated} post hoc by
temperature/Platt scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or isotonic
regression~\citep{zadrozny2002isotonic}. We use these as baselines: the honest question is not
whether the physics $\sigma$ beats a \emph{raw} overconfident head, but whether it beats a
\emph{properly recalibrated} one, and on which downstream task.

\paragraph{Equivariant trunks and chirality.} $E(3)$-equivariant
networks~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} are the de-facto trunks for
3D molecular property prediction, and the use of parity-\emph{odd} (pseudoscalar) irreps to
break achirality is standard in that literature; we make the architectural requirement precise
and verify it.

\paragraph{Acquisition and reward re-ranking.} Acquisition by the knowledge gradient~\citep{frazier2008kg}
and its Monte-Carlo implementations~\citep{balandat2020botorch,gardner2018gpytorch} drive edge
selection, while GFlowNets~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} sample candidates in
proportion to reward. We connect the per-edge sandwich variances to a Fisher--resistance graph
Laplacian (whose contrast variance is the resistance distance~\citep{klein1993resistance}) for
gauge-aware acquisition, and audit calibration's effect on selection and reward re-ranking (evaluable congeneric set, \texttt{eg5}) on the
OpenFF protein--ligand benchmark~\citep{hahn2022plbench}.

\section{The BAR information bottleneck}\label{sec:bar}
\paragraph{Setup.} For a single alchemical edge, forward and reverse works
$\{W^f_i\}_{i=1}^{n_f}$, $\{W^r_j\}_{j=1}^{n_r}$ define unified coordinates $x_i=W^f_i$,
$x_j=-W^r_j$. With $M=\ln(n_f/n_r)$ and $p(x;d)=s(x-d+M)$ (here $s(\cdot)$ is the logistic
function, distinct from the uncertainty $\sigma$ used later), the
BAR estimate $\dFh$ is the
root of the score $S(d)=\sum_{j\in r}p(x_j)-\sum_{i\in f}(1-p(x_i))$, and
$\partial_d S=-\sum p(1-p)=:-I(d)$, the curvature/overlap.

\begin{theorem}[$O(1)$ differentiable backward]\label{thm:back}
Let $\dFh(\theta)$ be defined by $S(\dFh,\theta)=0$ with non-degenerate curvature $I\neq0$
(positive overlap). The implicit function theorem gives $d\dFh/d\theta=I^{-1}\partial_\theta S$.
For window means, $d\dFh/d\mu^f=I^f/I$, $d\dFh/d\mu^r=-I^r/I$, with $|I^f/I|+|I^r/I|=1$. The
backward pass is one division by the curvature; the root-find is not unrolled.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sampling variance via the sandwich]\label{thm:sand}
$\dFh$ is an $M$-estimator; its asymptotic sampling variance is the sandwich
$\Var(\dFh)=A^{-1}BA^{-1}=B/I^2$, with $A=I$ and $B=n_f\Var_f[p]+n_r\Var_r[p]$
\citep{huber1967}. Here $A\neq B$ arises from BAR's \emph{stratified two-sample design}, not
from model misspecification; the sandwich is the sample-computable form and equals the
Bennett/Shirts--Chodera (MBAR) uncertainty to leading order in $1/n$
\citep{bennett1976bar,shirts2008mbar}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[scope of ``calibrated'']
$B/I^2$ is the variance of the \emph{sampling} error of $\dFh$, conditional on the works being
drawn from the correct alchemical ensembles. It says nothing about systematic force-field bias,
which is outside $B/I^2$ (we attach a separate epistemic/correction term in
Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The value of Theorem~\ref{thm:sand} for us is that this term is a
differentiable closed form (Theorem~\ref{thm:back}) and is non-negative by construction, where
the plug-in MBAR expression can return a negative value in a rare edge case
(very high overlap with tiny samples, where the plug-in $1/I$ undershoots the
$1/n_f+1/n_r$ correction), whereas $B/I^2\ge0$ always.
\end{remark}

The information equality $A=B$ (which would give $1/I$) holds for a single-sample MLE but fails
for BAR's two-sample design; we verify the gradients to machine precision and the variance to
within $1\%$ against $3000$-replicate Monte-Carlo and \texttt{pymbar} in the good-overlap
regime (at extreme low overlap, $n{=}20$, the bias-corrected plug-in runs $\sim$10\% below
MBAR, both $\approx1$).
The sandwich is derived for independent samples; on real FEP works both the sandwich inputs and the bootstrap truth are subsampled to the statistical inefficiency $g$~\citep{shirts2008mbar} before $B$ is formed, so the reported panel-B ratios use effective $n$.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:A}: the readout is correct; the learned foil is not (at any budget we tested).}
Figure~\ref{fig:A}A sweeps the work-distribution overlap. The sandwich $B/I^2$ coincides with
MBAR and Monte-Carlo truth across the range (reported/true se $\approx1$), with no Monte-Carlo.
We tested a \emph{fair} learned MVE head trained by Gaussian NLL, now fed work-summary
moments \emph{and} the overlap scalar $I$ (6-dim feature; reviewer M2a) at a realistic budget of
$\sim$200 edges and also at a large-budget oracle of 4000 training edges, twenty times larger.
The fair foil is still ${\approx}7\times$ overconfident at realistic budget (reported se
${\approx}0.15\times$ the truth; range $0.09$--$0.20\times$ across the overlap sweep), and
${\approx}5\times$ overconfident even at the large-budget oracle (se ${\approx}0.20\times$).
Corrected foils do not rescue the head at a realistic budget: a $\beta$-NLL
head~\citep{seitzer2022pitfalls} ($\beta{=}0.5$, the standard objective fix) does \emph{not}
recover calibration (se ${\approx}0.11\times$, no better than plain NLL), and a $5$-member deep
ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} reaches only the large-budget floor
(se ${\approx}0.20\times$, the same ${\approx}5\times$ overconfidence a $20\times$-larger-budget
plain head reaches). The residual $\sim$5$\times$ overconfidence is therefore a
\emph{data-efficiency} limit of a per-edge \emph{learned} variance, not an identifiability barrier
and not specific to the Gaussian-NLL objective: the conditional variance $se(\text{overlap})$ is
itself identifiable from single labels, pooling single-label edges by overlap recovering the
sandwich to $\sim$6\% at $4000$ edges ($\sim$4\% at $40000$). The bottleneck
\emph{computes} it exactly, per edge, differentiably, and at zero label cost, sidestepping the
learned estimator's budget floor entirely. The honest frame is
``learnable-with-data vs.\ exact-and-free''. Bare $1/I$ is shown only as the
information-equality plug-in it corrects ($1.08$--$2.65\times$, worst at high overlap), not as a
baseline anyone reports. Figure~\ref{fig:A}B checks calibration on real protein--ligand BACE1
RBFE binding edges (19 edges; sandwich/bootstrap-truth $1.00$, $[0.96,1.07]$); this bootstrap
reference reuses the same work samples (an internal consistency check). The genuine
independent-replicate validation is Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}: across $1145$ real binding edges from
the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks} (3 independent replicates
each), the reported MBAR se (equal to the sandwich $B/I^2$ to leading order, established in
Fig~\ref{fig:A}A) is calibrated-to-conservative against the across-replicate
truth (reported/replicate-SD $1.41$, CI $[1.26,1.62]$; $1.25\times$ after correcting the
$n{=}3$ small-sample SD bias, $\mathbb{E}[s]=c_4\sigma$, $c_4=0.886$; conservative on $72\%$
of edges and $28$ of $34$ targets).
The three replicates are independent stochastic repeats of the sampling process from identical
starting coordinates (OpenFE \texttt{protocol\_repeats}~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks});
the across-replicate SD therefore reflects sampling/convergence noise alone and is a
\emph{lower bound} on full reproducibility (it excludes pose and system-preparation variance).
The reported se does not \emph{systematically} under-state real
reproducibility, the opposite of the learned head's ${\approx}7\times$ overconfidence
at realistic budget (${\approx}5\times$ even at large budget).

\section{From a variance to a graph: gauge-aware acquisition}\label{sec:gauge}
\begin{theorem}[Fisher--resistance correspondence]\label{thm:fr}
For relative measurements $y_e=(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)+\varepsilon_e$,
$\varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,V_e)$ with $V_e=B_e/I_e^2$, the Gaussian posterior precision of
the node potentials is the weighted Laplacian $L=\sum_e w_e(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^\top$ with
conductances $w_e=1/V_e=I_e^2/B_e$. Contrast variance equals effective
resistance~\citep{klein1993resistance}, a single edge recovers $V_e$, Sherman--Morrison gives
$O(1)$ acquisition updates, and $\ker L\supseteq\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$, so the knowledge
gradient~\citep{frazier2008kg} is exactly zero on gauge/redundant directions: the acquisition is
automatically gauge-invariant.
\end{theorem}
The edge weights are the inverse sandwich variances: the differentiable readout of
Section~\ref{sec:bar} entering the decision layer, which a black-box uncertainty number cannot
provide. The gauge-zero property is structural: $\ker L=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$ for \emph{any} positive conductances, so KG$=0$ on gauge directions does not depend on the weight \emph{value}; and by Gauss--Markov the value does not improve ranking (Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The sandwich's contribution here is its differentiable closed form entering training and the trust calculation, not its magnitude. The Laplacian-precision and resistance-distance machinery is classical GLS-on-graphs and the resistance distance of \citet{klein1993resistance}; what is specific here is the \emph{sandwich} precision $I_e^2/B_e$ as the conductance (not raw overlap) and the automatic gauge-invariance of the acquisition. We make the correspondence precise, we do not claim new graph theory. Figure~\ref{fig:D} shows the knowledge gradient on the all-ones gauge contrast is
$10^{-21}$ (exact zero, an identifiability statement that transfers structurally to multi-target
networks), and that a gauge-\emph{unaware} acquisition wastes budget resolving a
decision-irrelevant cross-cluster offset, reaching the within-target top-$k$ at regret $0.27$
where the gauge-aware one reaches $0.01$. This experiment is a controlled simulation.

\section{The decisive payoff: calibrated cycle-closure quality control}\label{sec:qc}
The one downstream task where the calibrated variance's \emph{value}, not just its structure, is
load-bearing, and the paper's central demonstrated capability, is separating \emph{edge-level
systematic error} (non-convergence, hysteresis, charge/water/protonation artifacts) from
\emph{sampling noise} in a perturbation network. This is detection, not weighting of a fixed
estimate, so the Gauss--Markov ceiling that makes calibration second-order for sharpness and
ranking (the applicability map of Section~\ref{sec:audit}) does not apply here.

\paragraph{A calibrated cycle-closure test.} A perturbation network must close its cycles: the
signed sum of $\ddG$ around any loop is zero in expectation. Fitting node potentials by
generalized least squares with the sandwich conductances $w_e=I_e^2/B_e$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:fr})
gives residuals $r_e$ and the goodness-of-fit statistic $X^2=\sum_e r_e^2/V_e\sim\chi^2(\mathrm{dof})$,
with $\mathrm{dof}$ the number of independent cycles ($E$ minus the incidence rank). Under
correctly-sampled physics $\mathbb{E}[X^2/\mathrm{dof}]\approx1$; here it runs \emph{below} one
(median $0.34$), consistent with the conservative bars of Figure~\ref{fig:Arep} and, since
systematic error only \emph{raises} $\chi^2_\nu$, bounding them at ${\gtrsim}1.7\times$
conservative for closure residuals. A reduced $\chi^2$ well above one signals edge-level
systematic error. The test is (correctly) blind to
node-consistent force-field bias, which cancels around a loop. Network maximum-likelihood
estimation of $\ddG$ networks~\citep{xu2019diffnet} and cycle-closure hysteresis as a
diagnostic~\citep{wang2015fep} are standard; what is new is the \emph{calibrated} null: a
per-edge, replicate-validated aleatoric variance that gives the test per-edge-adaptive thresholds
and a false-positive rate controlled \emph{to the extent the per-edge null is calibrated}
(validated against independent replicates, Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}), in place of the field's fixed
hysteresis cutoffs. Because that null is conservative (median reduced $\chi^2=0.34$, so the bars
are ${\gtrsim}1.7\times$ wide for closure residuals), the nominal Benjamini--Hochberg rate is an
\emph{upper bound} on the true false-positive rate: the test under-flags rather than over-flags.
Crucially, its validity does not rest on the $\chi^2$ tail at all; the flags are confirmed by the
two distribution-free checks below (causal edge-removal and cross-replicate residual correlation),
which assume no Gaussian null.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:L}: a deployable detector whose usefulness \emph{is} $\sigma$-calibration.}
On the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks} ($1145$ edges over
$49$ systems, the $48$ with $\geq1$ independent cycle, $3$ replicates), the median reduced
$\chi^2$ is $0.34$ and a Benjamini--Hochberg test
(FDR $0.05$) flags six chemically-sensible systems (\texttt{brd4}, \texttt{bace}, \texttt{faah},
\texttt{cdk8}, \texttt{hif2a}, \texttt{p38}; Figure~\ref{fig:L}A; \texttt{brd4}'s data set is a
buried-water set, \texttt{bace} is protonation-sensitive). The detector's value is entirely a
function of calibration (Figure~\ref{fig:L}B): a uniform ${\times}0.15$ shrink standing in for the
learned head's central overconfidence (Figure~\ref{fig:A}) flags $88\%$ of systems (false-positive rate $\to1$,
useless), the calibrated sandwich flags $12\%$ (selective), and over-wide bars flag none, the
concrete decision an overconfident $\sigma$ silently corrupts and a calibrated one enables. The
three independent replicates confirm the flags are systematic, not sampling: pooling the
replicates shrinks the bars ($\mathrm{se}\to\mathrm{se}/\sqrt3$) yet \emph{increases} the flagged
systems' reduced $\chi^2$ toward the $3\times$ variance-components ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:L}C),
which occurs only for reproducible error (single-replicate $\chi^2_\nu=1+s^2/V_{\text{samp}}$ vs
pooled $1+3s^2/V_{\text{samp}}$; the ratio $\to3$ iff systematic-dominated).

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}: the flags are causal and reproduce out-of-sample.} Two
data-internal predictive checks, short of new MD. Greedily removing the flagged (highest-$|z|$)
edges restores consistency (reduced $\chi^2\le1$) in $3$--$7$ edges, versus $12$--$37$ removed at
random (Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}A): the flags name the \emph{causal} culprits. And fitting each
replicate's network separately, the per-edge standardized residuals correlate across independent
replicates ($r=+0.30$ to $+0.42$, $n=1143$; Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}B), so the flagged inconsistency
reproduces on unseen runs. Sampling noise would give zero.

\paragraph{Grounding against experiment, and the honest limit.} On \texttt{eg5}, whose ligands
carry ChEMBL identifiers, the network $\ddG$ (from the same GLS fit) agrees with real KIF11
experimental affinity~\citep{zdrazil2024chembl} at MUE $0.79$ kcal/mol ($95\%$ bootstrap CI
$[0.59,0.97]$, conditional on the fitted network; Pearson $R=0.65$ $[0.30,0.83]$, $p{\approx}2{\times}10^{-4}$, $n=28$), and its clean cycle-closure reduced
$\chi^2=0.64$ is consistent: a QC-clean system is
indeed accurate. Acting on the QC does \emph{not} improve accuracy here (internal $|z|$ vs
error-against-experiment: $r=+0.13$), exactly as the scope predicts: on a clean system the
residual error against experiment is dominated by node-consistent force-field bias, which cycle
closure cannot see. The complementary test, that repairing a \emph{flagged} system improves its
accuracy, requires experimental affinities for the flagged systems, which are anonymized in this
benchmark, and is left as the prospective next experiment (flag $\to$ re-run/repair $\to$
measure), now with named target edges.

\paragraph{Summary.} The calibrated bottleneck supplies, for free and differentiably, the null
model that turns FEP cycle-closure into a quality-control test that separates systematic from
sampling error on real networks, with a false-positive rate controlled by the calibrated null and
confirmed distribution-free, the sharpest use of the free calibration, and one an overconfident
learned $\sigma$ destroys.

\section{Chirality completeness}\label{sec:chiral}
An equivariant trunk~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} feeds the
bottleneck. Which readout channels does it need to distinguish stereoisomers?
\begin{theorem}[Chirality completeness]\label{thm:chiral}
Any $O(3)$-invariant readout (a function of pairwise distances, norms, or dot products) is
constant on an enantiomer pair $\{M,M^\ast\}$ ($M^\ast$ the mirror image) and is therefore
enantiomer-blind, so \emph{some} parity-odd channel is necessary. The pseudoscalar
$\chi(M)=\det[\mathbf r_2{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_3{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_4{-}\mathbf r_1]$
(the $\texttt{0o}$ triple product of four readout points $\mathbf r_i$) satisfies
$\chi(AM)=\det(A)\chi(M)$ for $A\in O(3)$, so it is $SO(3)$-invariant and flips sign under
reflection; $(\{\|\mathbf r_i{-}\mathbf r_j\|\},\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\chi)$ is a complete $SO(3)$
invariant (complete for distinguishing the enantiomer pair $\{M,\sigma M\}$ once the full distance set fixes the cloud up to $O(3)$; fully characterizing the chirality of a many-atom molecule needs a consistent set of pseudoscalars). Thus a single
$\texttt{0o}$ triple-product channel is a \emph{sufficient} minimal addition that restores the
chirality bit.
\end{theorem}
Consistent with the paper's principle, the chirality bit is \emph{read off} the geometry (a
pseudoscalar), not learned; this makes precise a fact familiar from the equivariant-network
literature (the role of odd irreps), stated here as an architectural-completeness requirement on
the trunk that feeds the bottleneck rather than as a central result. Figure~\ref{fig:E} realizes
it: an even readout gives bit-identical outputs on every
enantiomer pair (collapse $=0$ to machine precision), so its best possible chiral $\ddG$
prediction is zero; adding the $\texttt{0o}$ channel cuts enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE $11.7\times$
($1.67\to0.14$ kcal/mol).

\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figE_chirality_completeness.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{A parity-odd channel is necessary and one triple product suffices.}
(A) The even ($O(3)$) readout collapses enantiomers exactly; $\texttt{0o}$ separates them.
(B) Enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE drops $11.7\times$ when the $\texttt{0o}$ channel is added.}
\label{fig:E}
\end{figure}

\section{An applicability map: where this calibration helps downstream, and where it does not}\label{sec:audit}
Having established the primitive's decisive real-data payoff (the cycle-closure detector of
Section~\ref{sec:qc}), we now delimit, honestly, the downstream tasks it does \emph{not} improve.
This is an applicability map that scopes the free calibration to decisions and detection, not a
catalogue of failures. The free, differentiable $\sigma$ is attractive; the operative question is
whether it improves downstream machine learning. We test it on four tasks (interval sharpness,
edge ranking, commit-to-synthesis selection, and reward re-ranking over a measured congeneric set,
single target \texttt{eg5}) on the public OpenFF
protein--ligand benchmark~\citep{hahn2022plbench}, which aggregates the FEP+ and Merck data
sets~\citep{wang2015fep,schindler2020merck} (experimental $\ddG$ labels; both-endpoint
scaffold-disjoint out-of-distribution (OOD) splits), and in controlled simulations. The
amortized $\sigma$ we test (the decomposed physics $\sigma$, written
$\sigma_{\text{total}}$) is a deep-ensemble trunk's
$\sigma_{\text{total}}=q\sqrt{\sigma^2_{\text{ens}}+\sigma^2_{\text{ale}}}$: an epistemic
ensemble spread $\sigma_{\text{ens}}$ and a benchmark aleatoric floor $\sigma_{\text{ale}}$,
conformally scaled by $q$ at miscoverage $\alpha=0.1$ (90\% target coverage).

\paragraph{Trust: an overconfident $\sigma$ corrupts decisions; this decomposed $\sigma$ does not, and
calibration is cheap (on the OpenFF benchmark).} The clearest payoff of calibration is a trustworthy stop/commit
decision. Figure~\ref{fig:J}B isolates the effect of $\sigma$ by holding the (ensemble) mean
fixed and varying only the uncertainty used to commit a candidate to synthesis (commit iff the
lower-confidence bound (LCB) $\mu-\kappa\sigma\ge\tau$, with $\kappa=z_{1-\alpha}$ the
confidence multiplier). An overconfident epistemic-only
$\sigma$ over-claims badly: its realized-versus-claimed correctness shortfall is $+0.182$
(CI $[0.132,0.225]$) larger than the decomposed $\sigma$'s, whereas the decomposed physics
$\sigma$ is calibrated-to-conservative. Importantly, this trust is \emph{not unique} to
the physics $\sigma$: applying standard temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or
split-conformal recalibration to the same overconfident $\sigma$ recovers the trustworthiness
(temperature$-$ours $+0.034$, CI $[-0.018,0.081]$; conformal$-$ours $-0.029$, CI
$[-0.077,0.007]$; both indistinguishable from ours). The honest reading is that trustworthy
decisions require \emph{some} proper calibration, which the physics $\sigma$ supplies at no
calibration-set cost for the aleatoric term and, uniquely, as a differentiable graph weight.
Note also that even a \emph{conservative} bar carries a decision cost: a systematic
${\sim}1.25$--$1.41\times$ se inflation is itself a (safe-direction) miscalibration that, via
the LCB rule $\mu-\kappa\sigma\ge\tau$, biases toward late stopping and under-commit. It errs
safe against sampling noise; and because the independent-replicate SD is itself a lower bound
on full reproducibility (Section~\ref{sec:bar}, Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}), the inflation may be
closer to neutral against the true run-to-run spread once pose and system-preparation variance
are included.

\paragraph{Sharpness: this decomposed $\sigma$ is not sharper on real out-of-distribution residuals (OpenFF benchmark).} A natural hope is
that a decomposed, adaptive $\sigma$ yields tighter intervals than a flat conformal band at
equal coverage. On a controlled 1-D problem whose generative structure the decomposition knows,
it does (we keep that illustration in the supplement). On \emph{real} held-out trunk residuals
(Figure~\ref{fig:Breal}), at matched $\sim$0.90 marginal coverage, it does \emph{not}: the
decomposed interval is slightly \emph{wider} than flat split-conformal (width ratio
split/ours $0.92\times$), because the epistemic spread is small relative to the large
irreducible OOD error (correlation of half-width with $|$residual$|$ only $+0.11$). Its
conditional calibration is marginally better than a flat band and comparable to a fair
Mondrian-by-target conformal (per-target max coverage error $0.17$ vs $0.22$ for both split
and Mondrian), but the advantage is not significant
(ours$-$split $-0.044$, CI $[-0.121,0.005]$). The synthetic ``up to $2.2\times$ sharper vs split-conformal ($2.0\times$ vs CQR)'' result does
not transfer to real data.

\paragraph{Ranking and selection: the choice of $\sigma$ is second-order at matched budget.}
For edge ranking in active learning, sandwich versus naive edge weights differ by
$0.98\times$ $[0.93,1.03]$ on the primary calls-to-top-$k$ metric (24-seed bootstrap CI;
Figure~\ref{fig:C}, supplement): in a connected measurement graph the generalized-least-squares
(GLS) contrast mean is unbiased for \emph{any} positive weights (Gauss--Markov), so the weight
choice is second-order
for ranking. The same holds for commit selection: at matched commit \emph{volume}
(Figure~\ref{fig:J}A, ranking candidates by the standardized margin $(\mu-\tau)/\sigma$ and
committing the top fraction), the $\sigma$-aware ranking does not beat a raw-$\mu$ ranking
(precision at a 25\% budget $0.674$ vs $0.680$; trunk$-$raw $-0.006$, CI $[-0.016,0.007]$). A
calibrated bar tells you \emph{whether} to act; it does not change \emph{which} action ranks
first.

\paragraph{Reward re-ranking (evaluable congeneric set): this decomposed $\sigma$ does not help, and where $\sigma$ varies it hurts.} We drive
a trajectory-balance GFlowNet~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} over an evaluable ligand
space (a held-out target's measured congeneric series; trunk trained target-disjoint) with three
rewards: the calibrated LCB, the raw predicted value, and an overconfident
MVE reward. On \texttt{eg5}, the on-disk target with the \emph{largest} $\sigma$ spread (so the
calibrated reward genuinely re-ranks candidates), the calibrated reward \emph{lowers} the true
hit-rate of the generated distribution ($0.140$ vs raw $0.250$ and MVE $0.256$; cal$-$raw
$-0.110$, CI $[-0.151,-0.073]$; Figure~\ref{fig:K}). The mechanism is instructive:
$\mathrm{corr}(\sigma_{\text{total}},\text{affinity})=+0.42$: the high-$\sigma$ ligands are
the chemically novel \emph{strong} binders, so the LCB penalty $-\kappa\sigma$ demotes the true
winners. Calibration would help reward re-ranking only if $\sigma$ flagged \emph{low-quality}
candidates; on congeneric chemistry it flags \emph{novelty} instead. This is the steelman target
for calibrated reward re-ranking (the most $\sigma$-varying target available), and it fails. More usefully, it yields a transferable diagnostic
for calibrated reward re-ranking: an LCB reward helps only when $\sigma$ flags
\emph{low quality}; whenever $\sigma$ instead flags \emph{novelty}, the common case on
congeneric or lead-like series, the bound penalizes exactly the candidates worth making, and an
uncalibrated reward is the safer default. A regime where $\sigma$ tracks quality (e.g.\ diverse
libraries with genuine actives and decoys) is where calibrated re-ranking should pay, and is the
natural next test.

\paragraph{Where it pays: the stopping decision.} The one place a calibrated bar clearly pays is
knowing when to \emph{stop} an active-learning loop. Figure~\ref{fig:G} runs two learners with
the same correct posterior mean but different assumed uncertainty: the calibrated learner's
claimed confidence tracks actual top-$k$ correctness ($|\Delta|=0.022$) and stops at $30$ calls
$82\%$ correct, while an overconfident learner (using the learned head's $0.15\times$ scale, the ${\sim}7\times$ central overconfidence from Figure~\ref{fig:A}) stops twice as early at
only $60\%$ correct. This is a controlled simulation,
but it is the same lesson as the commit panel: calibration is a trust instrument.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} Five independent tests, namely sharpness, edge weighting, commit ranking,
reward re-ranking over a measured congeneric set (single target \texttt{eg5}), and (positively) stopping, paint one coherent picture. A calibrated per-edge
$\sigma$ does not sharpen intervals on real data, does not re-rank what to compute or commit,
and does not steer reward re-ranking toward better molecules on this evaluable congeneric set; an \emph{overconfident} $\sigma$,
however, silently corrupts the stop/commit decision. Calibration is therefore a
\emph{trust/decision instrument}, not a performance multiplier, and the physics bottleneck
delivers that calibration for free and differentiably. The differentiable graph weight earns its
place through \emph{structure}, not magnitude: it makes the gauge directions exactly
unidentifiable (knowledge gradient $=0$) and supplies the trust calculation, even though, by the
same Gauss--Markov argument that makes weighting second-order, the numerical \emph{value} of the
weight does not improve ranking. The value of reading the variance off the physics is therefore a
calibrated decision and an identifiable acquisition (and, decisively, the quality-control detector
of Section~\ref{sec:qc}), not a sharper or better-ranked prediction.

\section{Limitations}
The sandwich is aleatoric-only and conditional on correctly sampled ensembles; systematic
force-field bias is outside it, and the out-of-distribution behavior we audit rests on an
ensemble plus a scalar aleatoric floor (there is no learned force-field-correction head, so the
three-way decomposition of the synthetic illustration is not reproduced on real data). The
binding-edge se is validated against the three independent replicates of the OpenFE
IndustryBenchmarks2024 (Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}): in aggregate it is conservative by
$\sim$$1.4\times$ (raw ratio), or $\sim$$1.25\times$ after correcting the $n{=}3$
small-sample SD bias ($\mathbb{E}[s]=c_4\sigma$, $c_4=0.886$), though calibration is
heterogeneous per system ($28$ of $34$ targets conservative; one protonation-variant outlier
overconfident). The three replicates are seed/velocity-only repeats from identical starting
coordinates (OpenFE \texttt{protocol\_repeats}), so the across-replicate SD reflects
sampling/convergence noise and is a lower bound on full reproducibility (excluding pose and
system-preparation variance); the $\sim$$1.25$--$1.41\times$ conservatism may therefore be
closer to neutral against real reproducibility. The
gauge-acquisition and stopping experiments are controlled simulations. The reward re-ranking audit is a
single $\sigma$-varying target (\texttt{eg5}); the conclusion that $\sigma$ tracks novelty rather than quality
should be tested across more chemotypes. Point estimates also shift modestly run-to-run (torch CPU non-determinism), so the single-target re-ranking result is directional. The cycle-closure quality-control test (Section~\ref{sec:qc}) detects only \emph{edge-level} inconsistency (node-consistent force-field bias cancels around a loop and is invisible to it), and its flags are validated retrospectively (causal repair, held-out replicates) and against experiment on one clean system (\texttt{eg5}, where acting on the flags neither helps nor harms, as the scope predicts); the prospective flag$\to$repair$\to$remeasure test on a flagged system needs unblinded affinities or new MD. Finally, a structure-based (docking) reverse-screen we
built as a follow-on did not beat a ligand-similarity baseline on this benchmark, so the
structure-directed regime is left to future work rather than claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}
A differentiable BAR estimator at a surrogate's bottleneck makes the aleatoric uncertainty a
computed, differentiable, non-negative closed form rather than a learned guess. Its decisive
payoff is a \emph{calibrated cycle-closure quality-control test} that separates edge-level
systematic error from sampling noise on $1145$ real edges (Section~\ref{sec:qc}), with a
false-positive rate set by the calibrated null and confirmed by distribution-free causal and
cross-replicate checks, the sharpest use of the free calibration and one an overconfident learned
$\sigma$ destroys. The same primitive is usable as a Fisher--resistance graph weight (whose value
is structural, gauge-identifiability and trust, rather than a better-ranking magnitude) and is
paired with an exact chirality requirement. Tested honestly across the remaining downstream tasks,
this free calibration does not improve sharpness, ranking, or reward re-ranking over a measured
congeneric set (single target \texttt{eg5}); what it buys, beyond the quality-control test, is
trustworthy stop/commit decisions that an overconfident learned $\sigma$ destroys. Four results,
two new (the differentiable closed-form sandwich and its $O(1)$ backward) and two that make precise
or connect known results (the Fisher--resistance correspondence and the chirality-completeness
requirement), and a suite of falsifiable figures, including several
pre-declared, falsifiable negatives, delimit what the construction does and does not buy: in the regimes we
tested, calibration is a decision and quality-control instrument rather than a performance
multiplier; where the uncertainty would track quality rather than novelty, that verdict should be
revisited.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every figure regenerates from one command (the quality-control
figures via \texttt{make figL} and \texttt{make figLval}); the theorem constants and the
cycle-closure test are unit tests; all data are public (alchemtest BACE1/benzene, the OpenFF
protein--ligand benchmark, the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024 replicate
set~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks}, and ChEMBL~\citep{zdrazil2024chembl}). Recalibration
baselines use temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} and isotonic
regression~\citep{zadrozny2002isotonic}.
