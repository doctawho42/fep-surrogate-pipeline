\begin{abstract}
We turn a routine thermodynamic consistency check into a calibrated diagnostic for relative
binding free energy calculations. The Bennett Acceptance Ratio estimator that turns simulation
output into a free energy carries its own finite-sampling variance in closed form, computed from
the work samples rather than learned, with a backward pass no costlier than the forward pass. This per-edge error bar is
validated against independent molecular-dynamics replicates on $1145$ real protein-ligand
binding edges and found trustworthy against sampling and convergence noise. Its sharpest use is
detection: fitting cycle closure with these calibrated bars turns an ordinary consistency check
into a quality-control test flagging
a selective minority of perturbation networks as carrying systematic error. What the test
identifies reproduces out of sample: miscalibration measured on one set of replicates predicts the
closure statistic of a replicate it never saw. The ranking behind the flags is steadier than the
list of names flagged at a fixed threshold, and we report both. An overconfident learned
substitute for that error bar destroys the
test, flagging nearly every network regardless of whether it actually has a problem. The same
bottleneck yields a differentiable acquisition weight insensitive to gauge-redundant measurement
directions and a chirality-sensitive readout channel that distinguishes enantiomers. Audited
across further downstream uses, the calibrated bar sharpens no interval, changes no edge or
candidate ranking, and improves no reward-guided re-ranking; its value is confined to detection,
commit trust, and stopping decisions. Reading uncertainty off the physics is therefore a decision
and quality-control instrument, not a performance multiplier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Relative binding free energy calculations are the most accurate computational tool available for
ranking how tightly a set of candidate molecules will bind a target, and structure-based drug
discovery programs increasingly build them into decisions about which molecules to synthesize.
That accuracy comes at a price: each transformation between two related candidates, one edge of
a perturbation network, requires hours of molecular dynamics simulation, and a realistic project
touches dozens to hundreds of such edges. No program can run every edge to full convergence, so
triage is unavoidable: some edges get simulated first, some get simulated longer, and some
results get trusted enough to act on.

That triage is only as good as the error bar attached to each prediction. A result that looks
converged but is not will silently mislead a ranking of candidates; a result that is genuinely
well converged but reported with an inflated error bar wastes compute chasing a number that was
already trustworthy. A machine-learning surrogate built to speed up this triage inherits the
same requirement: it has to report not only a predicted binding free energy but how much to
trust it, because every downstream use of that number, choosing what to simulate next, deciding
when to stop simulating, and deciding which candidate to commit to synthesis, consumes the
uncertainty as directly as it consumes the prediction.

The prevailing way to obtain that uncertainty is to learn it, the same way the prediction itself
is learned: a head trained to output a mean and a variance~\citep{nix1994mve}, an ensemble of
independently trained models whose disagreement stands in for uncertainty~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles},
or a distribution-free wrapper calibrated after the fact on held-out
data~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}. All three treat uncertainty as something to be inferred
statistically from training examples. That approach overlooks a piece of the problem that is
already solved: the estimator that turns raw simulation output into a binding free energy for a
single edge, the Bennett Acceptance Ratio (BAR) estimator~\citep{bennett1976bar}, is a textbook
statistical estimator whose finite-sampling variance has a closed-form expression. That variance
is not a property of the surrogate model; it is a property of the estimator itself, and it is
computed directly from the same simulation output that produces the free-energy estimate,
without any additional training data.

This work takes that closed form as the uncertainty a screening pipeline should report: the
finite-sampling part is computed from the same simulation output rather than learned, at no extra
data cost, and it is packaged as a differentiable layer so that a surrogate can carry it at the
cost of the point estimate. The question this raises is not whether
such a computed error bar is theoretically correct, that follows from standard estimation
theory, but whether it is trustworthy in practice, and where trustworthiness actually matters in
a real screening workflow. We check the first question directly, against independent simulation
replicates on a large set of real protein-ligand binding calculations, and then trace the second
question through the rest of a screening pipeline: through an acquisition procedure that decides
what to simulate next, through a readout that has to tell molecules apart from their mirror
images, and, most consequentially, through a quality-control test that uses the calibrated error
bar to flag perturbation networks whose simulations disagree with each other more than sampling
noise can explain.

This paper makes the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item This paper reads the finite-sampling uncertainty of a relative binding free energy
estimate off a closed-form expression, the sandwich variance of the Bennett Acceptance Ratio
estimator, with a backward pass that costs a single division. It is implemented as an autograd
primitive and verified against finite differences, so the variance can be propagated through a
surrogate at the cost of the point estimate; the experiments here consume it as a number and as a
graph weight rather than by training through it.
\item This calibrated per-edge error bar, validated against independent simulation replicates on
real protein-ligand binding data, turns thermodynamic cycle closure into a quality-control test
that separates systematic simulation error from ordinary sampling noise, with the resulting
flags reproducing across independent simulation replicates.
\item The same per-edge variance forms the conductance of a measurement graph, producing an
acquisition weight whose knowledge gradient is exactly zero along every gauge-redundant
direction of that graph.
\item Adding a single parity-odd readout channel to an otherwise distance-and-angle-based
equivariant readout restores the readout's ability to distinguish a molecule from its mirror
image.
\item A systematic audit across interval sharpness, edge ranking, synthesis-commit selection,
and reward-guided re-ranking finds this calibrated uncertainty valuable only for
detection and stopping decisions, not for sharper or better-ranked predictions.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this article is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} places this
work against prior free-energy estimators, learned uncertainty, equivariant trunks, and
acquisition methods. Section~\ref{sec:bar} derives the closed-form sandwich variance and its
constant-cost backward pass. Section~\ref{sec:gauge} turns that variance into a gauge-aware
acquisition weight. Section~\ref{sec:qc} presents the calibrated cycle-closure quality-control
test on real binding data. Section~\ref{sec:chiral} establishes the chirality-completeness
requirement for the readout. Section~\ref{sec:audit} audits where the calibrated uncertainty
helps downstream learning and where it does not. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} collects the
limitations of the approach, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Free-energy estimators.} BAR~\citep{bennett1976bar} and its multistate
generalization MBAR~\citep{shirts2008mbar} are the standard estimators for alchemical free
energies; FEP+~\citep{wang2015fep} brought them to prospective potency prediction, and large
retrospective assessments~\citep{schindler2020merck} and best-practice guides~\citep{mey2020alchem}
document both their accuracy and their per-edge uncertainty, the sandwich variance of
$M$-estimation~\citep{huber1967,white1982sandwich}.

\paragraph{Learned and recalibrated uncertainty.} The prevailing alternative is to learn
predictive uncertainty: a mean--variance estimation (MVE) head by Gaussian
NLL~\citep{nix1994mve}, a deep
ensemble for the epistemic term~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles}, the aleatoric/epistemic
split~\citep{kendall2017uncertainties}, or distribution-free conformal
prediction~\citep{angelopoulos2021conformal}, including quantile-adaptive variants such as
CQR~\citep{romano2019cqr}. A predicted $\sigma$ can also be \emph{recalibrated} post hoc by
temperature/Platt scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or isotonic
regression~\citep{zadrozny2002isotonic}.

\paragraph{Equivariant trunks and chirality.} $E(3)$-equivariant
networks~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} are the de-facto trunks for
3D molecular property prediction, and the use of parity-\emph{odd} (pseudoscalar) irreps to
break achirality is standard in that literature.

\paragraph{Acquisition and reward re-ranking.} Acquisition by the knowledge gradient~\citep{frazier2008kg}
and its Monte-Carlo implementations~\citep{balandat2020botorch,gardner2018gpytorch} drive edge
selection, while GFlowNets~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} sample candidates in
proportion to reward; the resistance distance of a weighted graph Laplacian equals contrast
variance in a Gaussian graphical model~\citep{klein1993resistance}.

The works cited above use that uncertainty as a reported number or as a separately learned model.
This work supplies the same sandwich variance as a differentiable quantity, uses it as the
conductance of a gauge-aware acquisition graph, and puts it to a further, distinct
use as the calibrated null of a real-data cycle-closure quality-control test.

\section{The BAR information bottleneck}\label{sec:bar}
\paragraph{Setup.} For a single alchemical edge, forward and reverse works
$\{W^f_i\}_{i=1}^{n_f}$, $\{W^r_j\}_{j=1}^{n_r}$ define unified coordinates $x_i=W^f_i$,
$x_j=-W^r_j$. With $M=\ln(n_f/n_r)$ and $p(x;d)=s(x-d+M)$ (here $s(\cdot)$ is the logistic
function, distinct from the uncertainty $\sigma$ used later), the
BAR estimate $\dFh$ is the
root of the score $S(d)=\sum_{j\in r}p(x_j)-\sum_{i\in f}(1-p(x_i))$, and
$\partial_d S=-\sum p(1-p)=:-I(d)$, the curvature/overlap.

\begin{theorem}[$O(1)$ differentiable backward]\label{thm:back}
Let $\dFh(\theta)$ be defined by $S(\dFh,\theta)=0$ with non-degenerate curvature $I\neq0$
(positive overlap). The implicit function theorem gives $d\dFh/d\theta=I^{-1}\partial_\theta S$.
For window means, $d\dFh/d\mu^f=I^f/I$, $d\dFh/d\mu^r=-I^r/I$, with $|I^f/I|+|I^r/I|=1$. The
backward pass is one division by the curvature; the root-find is not unrolled.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sampling variance via the sandwich]\label{thm:sand}
$\dFh$ is an $M$-estimator; its asymptotic sampling variance is the sandwich
$\Var(\dFh)=A^{-1}BA^{-1}=B/I^2$, with $A=I$ and $B=n_f\Var_f[p]+n_r\Var_r[p]$
\citep{huber1967}. Here $A\neq B$ arises from BAR's \emph{stratified two-sample design}, not
from model misspecification; the sandwich is the sample-computable form and agrees with the
Bennett/Shirts--Chodera (MBAR) uncertainty
\citep{bennett1976bar,shirts2008mbar}. Concretely, the plug-in MBAR/Bennett uncertainty is
$\Var_{\text{MBAR}}=1/I-(1/n_f+1/n_r)$ (identical to \texttt{pymbar}'s \texttt{MBAR}
uncertainty). Evaluated at the root, with works drawn from the correct pair of ensembles, this
is not an approximation to the sandwich but the same quantity: $B/I^2=1/I-(1/n_f+1/n_r)$
identically, so the $-(1/n_f+1/n_r)$ term is precisely the $O(1/n)$ gap between the naive
information-equality plug-in $1/I$ and the sandwich. The two \emph{sample} plug-ins differ only
through the moments they substitute, and that difference shrinks with $n$ (paired
Monte-Carlo check against \texttt{bar.estimator.bar\_estimate},
\texttt{tests/test\_estimator.py::test\_mbar\_sandwich\_discrepancy\_shrinks\_with\_n}: the
mean paired $|\Var_{\text{MBAR}}-B/I^2|$ falls monotonically and by more than an order of
magnitude from $n{=}40$ to $n{=}320$, the range this repo verifies; beyond that the paired
discrepancy is smaller than Monte-Carlo noise at a modest replicate budget, so we do not quote
a range past $320$). This is why bare $1/I$ (Figure~\ref{fig:A},
naive) overstates the calibrated uncertainty and \texttt{pymbar}'s reported MBAR se does not.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[scope of ``calibrated'']
$B/I^2$ is the variance of the \emph{sampling} error of $\dFh$, conditional on the works being
drawn from the correct alchemical ensembles. It says nothing about systematic force-field bias,
which is outside $B/I^2$ (we attach a separate epistemic/correction term in
Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The value of Theorem~\ref{thm:sand} for us is that this term is a
differentiable closed form (Theorem~\ref{thm:back}) and is non-negative by construction, where
the plug-in MBAR expression can return a negative value in a rare edge case
(very high overlap with tiny samples, where the plug-in $1/I$ undershoots the
$1/n_f+1/n_r$ correction), whereas $B/I^2\ge0$ always.
\end{remark}

The information equality $A=B$ (which would give $1/I$) holds for a single-sample MLE but fails
for BAR's two-sample design; we verify the gradients to machine precision and the variance to
within $1\%$ against $3000$-replicate Monte-Carlo and \texttt{pymbar} in the good-overlap
regime (at extreme low overlap, $n{=}20$, the bias-corrected plug-in runs $\sim$10\% below
MBAR, both $\approx1$).
The sandwich is derived for independent samples; on real FEP works both the sandwich inputs and the bootstrap truth are subsampled to the statistical inefficiency $g$~\citep{shirts2008mbar} before $B$ is formed, so the reported panel-B ratios use effective $n$.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:A}: the readout is correct; the learned foil is not (at any budget we tested).}
Figure~\ref{fig:A}A sweeps the work-distribution overlap. The sandwich $B/I^2$ coincides with
MBAR and Monte-Carlo truth across the range (reported/true se $\approx1$), with no Monte-Carlo.
We tested a \emph{fair} learned MVE head trained by Gaussian NLL, now fed work-summary
moments \emph{and} the overlap scalar $I$ (6-dim feature) at a realistic budget of
$\sim$200 edges and also at a large-budget oracle of 4000 training edges, twenty times larger.
The fair foil is still ${\approx}7\times$ overconfident at realistic budget (reported se
${\approx}0.15\times$ the truth; range $0.09$--$0.20\times$ across the overlap sweep), and
${\approx}5\times$ overconfident even at the large-budget oracle (se ${\approx}0.20\times$).
Corrected foils do not rescue the head at a realistic budget: a $\beta$-NLL
head~\citep{seitzer2022pitfalls} ($\beta{=}0.5$, the standard objective fix) does \emph{not}
recover calibration (se ${\approx}0.11\times$, in fact slightly worse than plain NLL's
$0.15\times$), and at this training seed a $5$-member deep
ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} reaches only the large-budget floor
(se ${\approx}0.20\times$, the same ${\approx}5\times$ overconfidence a $20\times$-larger-budget
plain head reaches). These ratios are stable across independent training seeds for the plain and
$\beta$-NLL heads: over $5$ seeds the plain head spans $0.1229$--$0.2103\times$ (mean
$0.1537\times$) and the $\beta$-NLL head $0.1024$--$0.1256\times$ (mean $0.1147\times$), so their
overconfidence is a property of the estimator at this budget rather than of one lucky
initialization. The $5$-member ensemble is not stable: across the same $5$ seeds, each with a
disjoint $5$-network member block, it spans $0.1984$--$1.2200\times$ (mean $0.4198\times$, sd
$0.4481\times$), and one seed \emph{overestimates} the true se by $22\%$, crossing perfect
calibration. Unlike the plain, oracle ($0.1941$--$0.2571\times$, mean $0.2101\times$), and
$\beta$-NLL heads, whose overconfidence is robust across independent retrains, the ensemble is
unreliable in \emph{both} directions at this budget,
so a learned per-edge variance is not a trustworthy substitute for the closed-form sandwich either
way. The residual $\sim$5$\times$ overconfidence of the plain, oracle, and $\beta$-NLL heads is
therefore a
\emph{data-efficiency} limit of a per-edge \emph{learned} variance, not an identifiability barrier
and not specific to the Gaussian-NLL objective: the conditional variance $se(\text{overlap})$ is
itself identifiable from single labels, pooling single-label edges by overlap recovering the
sandwich to $\sim$6\% at $4000$ edges ($\sim$4\% at $40000$). The bottleneck
\emph{computes} it exactly, per edge, differentiably, and at zero label cost, sidestepping the
learned estimator's budget floor entirely. The comparison is therefore
learnable-with-data versus exact-and-free. Bare $1/I$ is shown only as the
information-equality plug-in it corrects ($1.08$--$2.65\times$, worst at high overlap), not as a
baseline anyone reports. Figure~\ref{fig:A}B checks calibration on real protein--ligand BACE1
RBFE binding edges (19 edges; sandwich/bootstrap-truth $1.00$, $[0.96,1.07]$); this bootstrap
reference reuses the same work samples (an internal consistency check). On those same edges the
naive $1/I$ overstates the bootstrap se by $2$--$12\times$ (mean $6.07$, $[2.78,12.01]$), with
every edge sitting at overlap $\geq0.84$ (per-edge values, Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:bace1}).
The genuine independent-replicate validation is Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}. Each edge's reported bar
is composed in quadrature from the complex-leg and solvent-leg MBAR uncertainties shipped with
the benchmark, $\mathrm{se}=\sqrt{\mathrm{se}_{\text{complex}}^2+\mathrm{se}_{\text{solvent}}^2}$;
each leg term is a multistate MBAR uncertainty over that leg's $\lambda$ windows, not the
two-state BAR quantity of Theorem~\ref{thm:sand}, so what is validated here is the bar as a
practitioner receives it rather than the two-state identity itself. Across $1145$ real
binding edges from
the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks} (3 independent replicates
each), the reported MBAR se is calibrated-to-conservative against the across-replicate
truth (reported/replicate-SD $1.41$, CI $[1.26,1.62]$; conservative on $72\%$
of edges and $28$ of $34$ targets). Both sides of that ratio pool in quadrature, so the
$n{=}3$ replicate term is unbiased at the variance level and needs no small-sample
correction.
The three replicates are independent stochastic repeats of the sampling process from identical
starting coordinates (OpenFE \texttt{protocol\_repeats}~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks});
the across-replicate SD therefore reflects sampling/convergence noise alone and is a
\emph{lower bound} on full reproducibility (it excludes pose and system-preparation variance).
The reported se does not \emph{systematically} under-state real
reproducibility, the opposite of the learned head's ${\approx}7\times$ overconfidence
at realistic budget (${\approx}5\times$ even at large budget).

\section{From a variance to a graph: gauge-aware acquisition}\label{sec:gauge}
\begin{theorem}[Fisher--resistance correspondence]\label{thm:fr}
For relative measurements $y_e=(\varphi_j-\varphi_i)+\varepsilon_e$,
$\varepsilon_e\sim\mathcal N(0,V_e)$ with $V_e=B_e/I_e^2$, the Gaussian posterior precision of
the node potentials is the weighted Laplacian $L=\sum_e w_e(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^\top$ with
conductances $w_e=1/V_e=I_e^2/B_e$. Contrast variance equals effective
resistance~\citep{klein1993resistance}, a single edge recovers $V_e$, Sherman--Morrison gives
$O(1)$ acquisition updates, and $\ker L\supseteq\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$, so the knowledge
gradient (KG)~\citep{frazier2008kg} is exactly zero on gauge/redundant directions: the acquisition is
automatically gauge-invariant.
\end{theorem}
The edge weights are the inverse sandwich variances: the differentiable readout of
Section~\ref{sec:bar} entering the decision layer, which a black-box uncertainty number cannot
provide. The gauge-zero property is structural: KG$=0$ on gauge directions follows from the inclusion alone and so does not depend on the weight \emph{value}. For a \emph{connected} network the inclusion is an equality, $\ker L=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf 1\}$, for any positive conductances, and the overall additive offset is then the only unidentifiable direction; a network with $k$ components has a $k$-dimensional kernel, one offset per component. and by Gauss--Markov the value does not improve ranking (Section~\ref{sec:audit}). The sandwich's contribution here is its differentiable closed form entering training and the trust calculation, not its magnitude. The Laplacian-precision and resistance-distance machinery is classical generalized least squares (GLS) on graphs and the resistance distance of \citet{klein1993resistance}; what is specific here is the \emph{sandwich} precision $I_e^2/B_e$ as the conductance (not raw overlap) and the automatic gauge-invariance of the acquisition. The graph theory it rests on is standard. A controlled simulation
(Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:D}) shows the knowledge gradient on the all-ones gauge contrast is
$10^{-21}$ (exact zero, an identifiability statement that transfers structurally to multi-target
networks), and that a gauge-\emph{unaware} acquisition wastes budget resolving a
decision-irrelevant cross-cluster offset, reaching the within-target top-$k$ at regret $0.27$
where the gauge-aware one reaches $0.01$.

\section{The sharpest use: calibrated cycle-closure quality control}\label{sec:qc}
The one downstream task where the calibrated variance's \emph{value}, not just its structure, is
load-bearing, and the paper's central demonstrated capability, is separating \emph{edge-level
systematic error} (non-convergence, hysteresis, charge/water/protonation artifacts) from
\emph{sampling noise} in a perturbation network. That separation is the calibrated
quality-control (QC) test of this section. The calibration underwriting this test is
validated against seed and velocity replicate spread, a lower bound on full run-to-run
reproducibility (it excludes pose and system-preparation variance). This is \emph{detection}, not weighting of a fixed
estimate: the Gauss--Markov ceiling that makes the calibrated variance second-order elsewhere (in a
connected graph the GLS contrast mean is unbiased for \emph{any} positive weights, so the weight
\emph{value} cannot improve ranking, as the applicability map of Section~\ref{sec:audit} develops)
does not bind a goodness-of-fit test, whose statistic depends on the weights directly.

\paragraph{A calibrated cycle-closure test.} A perturbation network must close its cycles: the
signed sum of $\ddG$ around any loop is zero in expectation. Fitting node potentials by
generalized least squares with conductances $w_e=1/V_e$, which Theorem~\ref{thm:fr} identifies
with the sandwich precision $I_e^2/B_e$ whenever the work samples are available (on this benchmark
they are not released, so $V_e$ is the reported per-edge variance composed from the two legs,
Section~\ref{sec:methods}),
gives residuals $r_e$ and the goodness-of-fit statistic $X^2=\sum_e r_e^2/V_e\sim\chi^2(\mathrm{dof})$,
with $\mathrm{dof}$ the number of independent cycles ($E$ minus the incidence rank). Under
correctly-sampled physics $\mathbb{E}[X^2/\mathrm{dof}]\approx1$; here it runs \emph{below} one
(median $0.34$), qualitatively consistent with the conservative bars of Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}.
We do not convert that median into a conservatism factor. The quantity the closure statistic
identifies is not a median: writing the reported variance as $V_e^{\text{rep}}=c_e^2V_e$, whitening
by the reported errors gives $\mathbb{E}[X^2]=\operatorname{tr}(MD)$ with $D=\mathrm{diag}(c_e^{-2})$,
so by Theorem~\ref{thm:cons} $\mathbb{E}[\chi^2_\nu]$ is the curl-leverage-weighted \emph{mean} of
$c_e^{-2}$. Inverting a median recovers a scalar conservatism only when $c_e$ is constant across
edges, and Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}B shows it is not. A replicate-validated ratio and a
closure-implied one are therefore different functionals of a heavy-tailed per-edge ratio
distribution, and no single scalar conservatism factor is identified by these data; the apparent
disagreement between them is an artefact of comparing different functionals rather than a physical
discrepancy. Residual correlation among edges sharing a ligand endpoint, which the diagonal-$V$ GLS
does not model, is separately negligible (excess correlation over the projector-induced
null $-0.0079$ $[-0.0314,+0.0164]$). The Benjamini--Hochberg flag set is unchanged. A reduced
$\chi^2$ well above one signals edge-level systematic error. The test is (correctly) blind to
node-consistent force-field bias, which cancels around a loop.

\begin{theorem}[Estimation--detection conservation law]\label{thm:cons}
In the whitened GLS fit let $M=I-H$ be the residual-maker projector onto the cycle space. Its
diagonal, the curl-leverage $h_e=M_{ee}$, satisfies $h_e+w_e\Omega_e=1$ with $w_e=1/V_e$ and
$\Omega_e$ the effective resistance of edge $e$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:fr}); hence
$\sum_e h_e=\mathrm{dof}$, and the closure-$\chi^2$ noncentrality against a systematic shift $\mu$
on edge $e$ is $h_e\mu^2/V_e$. A bridge edge (in no cycle) has $h_e=0$ and is undetectable at any
$\mu$; a node-consistent (gradient) bias lies in $\mathrm{range}(H)$ and is annihilated by $M$.
The QC's blindness to node-consistent force-field bias is therefore a \emph{derived} property, not
an assumption, and $h_e$ is a per-edge observability certificate with detectable-shift resolution
$\delta^\ast_e=\sqrt{V_e/h_e}$.
\end{theorem}
The algebra is standard and we claim none of it: $h_e=M_{ee}$ is the hat-matrix leverage of the
whitened design, its complement $w_e\Omega_e$ is the classical correspondence between leverage and
effective resistance~\citep{klein1993resistance}, and $\sum_e h_e=\operatorname{tr}(I-H)=\mathrm{dof}$
is the trace identity for a projector. What the theorem adds is the reading: $h_e$ is a per-edge
observability certificate, it makes the test's blindness to node-consistent bias a derived
consequence rather than a modelling assumption, and it says in advance which edges of a given
network can carry no evidence at all.
The resulting observability map lists the structurally un-auditable (bridge) edges per system; on this benchmark the
curl-leverage does not additionally predict the \emph{location} of reproducible systematic error
(studentized Spearman $\rho=-0.073$, permutation $p=0.9571$, not significant; Supplementary
Figure~\ref{fig:Lev}), so we claim only the structural certificate.

Network maximum-likelihood
estimation of $\ddG$ networks~\citep{xu2019diffnet}, cycle-closure hysteresis as a
diagnostic~\citep{wang2015fep,cui2020hysteresis}, weighted cycle closure~\citep{li2023wcc}, and the
constrained variational solution of a whole network under loop closure~\citep{giese2021variational}
are established; what is new is the \emph{calibrated} null: a
per-edge, replicate-validated aleatoric variance that gives the test per-edge-adaptive thresholds
and a false-positive rate controlled \emph{to the extent the per-edge null is calibrated}
(validated against independent replicates, Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}), complementing the field's fixed
hysteresis cutoffs, together with the cross-replicate reproduction check of Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}B and the
estimation--detection conservation law of Theorem~\ref{thm:cons}, which fixes which edges are
auditable at all; what is shared with the works above is the weighted least-squares treatment of the
cycle graph itself. On the same $1145$ edges, flagging by the
calibrated per-edge null does not measurably out-discriminate a fixed $1.0$ kcal/mol hysteresis
cutoff or a fixed-$\mathrm{se}$ $\chi^2$ test, scored against edges whose sign and magnitude
reproduce across independent replicates (AUC
$0.865$ vs $0.575$ and $0.725$; paired bootstrap difference $+0.140$ $[-0.008,+0.316]$,
Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:Cut}). The calibrated null's advantages are therefore structural, namely
per-edge-adaptive thresholds, a false-positive rate tied to a replicate-validated null, and the
observability map of Theorem~\ref{thm:cons}, rather than a demonstrated gain in discrimination on
this benchmark. The evidence that the flags are real remains the distribution-free cross-replicate
reproduction check, which does not depend on the
$\chi^2$ scaling. This retrospective test needs only the calibrated per-edge $se$ as shipped
with the benchmark; it does \emph{not} require the
differentiability or $O(1)$ backward of Theorems~\ref{thm:back}--\ref{thm:sand}. Those results
supply the layer that makes end-to-end propagation possible and bound its cost, but no experiment
reported here trains through it: what the real-data work consumes is the variance as a number, in
the detector, and as the acquisition graph weight of Section~\ref{sec:gauge}. The detector's novelty is
the \emph{calibrated null}, not the differentiable machinery. That null is conservative in the bulk (median reduced $\chi^2=0.34$, so the bars
are ${\gtrsim}1.7\times$ wide for closure residuals), so on a typical system the test under-flags.
That observation does not bound the realized false-positive rate, and we do not read it as doing
so: Benjamini--Hochberg controls the false discovery rate through the behaviour of the null
$p$-values near the rejection threshold, not through the median of the statistic, and the
per-system calibration of Figure~\ref{fig:Arep} is heterogeneous enough that a conservative bulk
and an anti-conservative tail can coexist. The nominal level is reported as nominal. The case for
the flags therefore rests not on the $\chi^2$ tail but on the
distribution-free cross-replicate reproduction check below,
which assumes no Gaussian null. A subtler concern is that four of the six flagged systems are
themselves locally overconfident against their own across-replicate spread
(Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}: \texttt{brd4} $0.76\times$, \texttt{faah} $0.90\times$, \texttt{bace}
$0.96\times$, \texttt{hif2a} $0.96\times$; raw $n{=}3$ SD ratios, whose residual small-sample
bias is upward and target-specific, of order the reciprocal of the edge count, so that debiasing
moves all four \emph{further} below one rather than toward it, and the raw ratios used
below are if anything generous to these systems, not the reverse), so a flag could partly reflect
that system's own too-tight bars rather than genuine physical inconsistency; inflating each such
system's own null by the reciprocal of its own measured (raw) ratio (Supplementary
Table~\ref{tab:P8}) leaves all six flags in place, each self-calibrated reduced $\chi^2$
($2.36$--$8.97$) remaining far above the level a sampling-consistent network of this size and
degree-of-freedom profile shows.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:L}: a deployable detector whose usefulness \emph{is} $\sigma$-calibration.}
On the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks} ($1145$ edges over
$49$ systems, the $48$ with $\geq1$ independent cycle, $3$ replicates), the median reduced
$\chi^2$ is $0.34$ and a Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR)
test at $0.05$ applied to the first replicate flags six chemically-sensible systems
(\texttt{brd4}, \texttt{bace}, \texttt{faah},
\texttt{cdk8}, \texttt{hif2a}, \texttt{p38}; Figure~\ref{fig:L}A; \texttt{brd4}'s data set is a
buried-water set, \texttt{bace} is protonation-sensitive). That particular set of six is a
single-replicate draw and should not be read as a census; Section~\ref{sec:stability} measures how
much of it survives the other two replicates. The detector's value is entirely a
function of calibration (Figure~\ref{fig:L}B): a uniform ${\times}0.15$ shrink standing in for the
learned head's central overconfidence (Figure~\ref{fig:A}) flags $88\%$ of systems (false-positive rate $\to1$,
useless), the calibrated sandwich flags $12\%$ (selective), and over-wide bars flag none, the
concrete decision an overconfident $\sigma$ silently corrupts and a calibrated one enables. That
uniform shrink is a stress test rather than a realistic head. Applying instead the learned head's
\emph{measured}, overlap-dependent miscalibration profile per edge (rank-transferred onto each
edge's own overlap, Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:P4}) flags $42$ of $48$ systems against the
calibrated sandwich's $6$ ($7.00\times$), so the degradation survives a heterogeneous, non-uniform
profile and is not an artifact of the uniform factor. A shuffled-profile control, the identical
multiset of per-edge ratios with the association to overlap destroyed, also flags $42$ of $48$:
because the profile's entire measured range ($0.09$--$0.20\times$) is deeply overconfident, even
its mildest value inflates an edge's $\chi^2$ contribution ${\sim}25\times$, so once a network's
edges are all shrunk somewhere in that range, whether it crosses the Benjamini--Hochberg threshold
is guaranteed by the ratio ceiling alone: every ratio is at most $0.20$, so every system's $\chi^2$
contribution inflates by at least $25\times$, which saturates the test regardless of which edges
get more or less of it within that range. It is
therefore the \emph{magnitude} of the learned head's miscalibration, not the \emph{uniformity} of
the Figure~\ref{fig:L}B stand-in, that destroys the test, and the heterogeneous, overlap-dependent
structure of a profile this overconfident carries no additional information for it. The
demonstration is therefore scoped to heads as overconfident as the measured one (reported
$\mathrm{se}$ $5$--$11\times$ too small); it establishes that such heads saturate the test, not the
shrink magnitude at which selectivity is first lost. Sweeping the shrink magnitude supplies that
missing curve (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:P4b}), which proves graded rather than
threshold-like, so the meaningful readout is where a majority is flagged rather than where
flagging first increases: the flagged fraction climbs from the calibrated
$6/48$ ($12\%$) through a graded mid-curve rise, $12/48$ ($25\%$) at a $0.7\times$ scale to $30/48$
($62\%$) at $0.3\times$, reaching half the $48$-system benchmark at $s_{50}=0.3\times$, and the
learned head's measured miscalibration band ($0.09$--$0.20\times$) lies below that point, on the
deep-shrink side of where the test already flags a majority of systems. The quality-control test is
therefore selective over roughly a factor of three of $\sigma$ under-estimation below the
calibrated scale; over-wide $\sigma$ costs power in the other direction as well ($1.3\times$ flags
only $1/48$, and $1.5\times$ and $2.0\times$ flag none), so the calibrated scale is a two-sided
optimum rather than a plateau, and the head we measured is not marginal but well inside the
saturated regime.
The
three independent replicates confirm the flags are systematic, not sampling: pooling the
replicates shrinks the bars ($\mathrm{se}\to\mathrm{se}/\sqrt3$) yet \emph{increases} the flagged
systems' reduced $\chi^2$ toward the $3\times$ variance-components ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:L}C),
which occurs only for reproducible error (single-replicate $\chi^2_\nu=1+s^2/V_{\text{samp}}$ vs
pooled $1+3s^2/V_{\text{samp}}$; the ratio $\to3$ iff systematic-dominated).

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}: the flagged inconsistency reproduces on unseen runs.} Greedily
removing the flagged (highest-$|z|$) edges restores consistency (reduced $\chi^2\le1$) in $3$--$7$
edges, versus $12$--$37$ removed at random (Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}A). We report this as an
illustration of the repair procedure and not as evidence, because it cannot fail: the statistic
being driven down is $X^2=\sum_e r_e^2/V_e$ and the removal order is descending $z_e^2$, which is
that sum's own per-edge summand, so a networked world with no localized systematic error whatever
reproduces the same contrast. The evidential check is the out-of-sample one. Fitting each
replicate's network separately, the per-edge standardized residuals correlate across independent
replicates ($r=+0.30$ to $+0.42$, $n=1143$; Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}B), and the correlation is
concentrated in the flagged systems, so what the flags identify is reproducible across runs rather
than a property of one draw. Sampling noise would give zero.

\paragraph{Grounding against experiment, and where the flags stop helping.} On \texttt{eg5}, whose ligands
carry ChEMBL identifiers, the network $\ddG$ (from the same GLS fit) agrees with real KIF11
experimental affinity~\citep{zdrazil2024chembl} at a mean unsigned error (MUE) of $0.79$ kcal/mol ($95\%$ bootstrap CI
$[0.59,0.97]$, conditional on the fitted network; Pearson $R=0.65$ $[0.30,0.83]$, $p{\approx}2{\times}10^{-4}$, $n=28$), and its clean cycle-closure reduced
$\chi^2=0.64$ is consistent: a QC-clean system is
indeed accurate. Acting on the QC does \emph{not} improve accuracy here (internal $|z|$ vs
error-against-experiment: $r=+0.13$), exactly as the scope predicts: on a clean system the
residual error against experiment is dominated by node-consistent force-field bias, which cycle
closure cannot see. The complementary test, that repairing a \emph{flagged} system improves its
accuracy, needs experimental affinities for the flagged systems; these are not directly tabulated
in the replicate set but are recoverable by per-paper ChEMBL structure-search. Of
the six flagged systems, four clear a $60\%$ public-ChEMBL coverage gate (\texttt{cdk8, hif2a, p38,
bace}; connectivity-based structure matching, one assay per system, IC50 before Ki, never mixed);
their baseline network MUE against this grounded experimental affinity is $0.79$--$1.47$ kcal/mol,
typical-magnitude FEP error, confirming the grounding itself is sound. On these four, guided
removal of the flagged high-$|z|$ edges (the same repair order as Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}A, fixing a
removal count $K=1$--$3$ per system) does \emph{not} reduce MUE-vs-experiment more than an
equal-size random removal (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:Lcausal}; per-system one-sided permutation
$p=0.905,0.136,0.880,0.071$; combined Stouffer $p=0.48$, sign test $p=0.69$, pre-registered success
required Stouffer $p<0.05$): two systems improve slightly over their random-null mean and two
worsen slightly, a wash with no system individually significant. This is the now
experiment-grounded, multi-system generalization of the \texttt{eg5} result above, not a
contradiction of the QC's value: cycle closure tests \emph{edge-level} consistency (signed $\ddG$
sums to zero around a loop) and is blind to node-consistent force-field bias, which a smooth
per-ligand offset cancels around any cycle, and it is exactly that bias that dominates residual
MUE-vs-experiment on real, flagged, multi-system data. The remaining two flagged systems
(\texttt{brd4, faah}) did not clear the coverage gate and are not included; the fully prospective
flag $\to$ repair $\to$ remeasure test, which needs new MD rather than a retrospective affinity
join, is left as future work.

\paragraph{Summary.} The calibrated bottleneck supplies, for free and differentiably, the null
model that turns FEP cycle-closure into a quality-control test that separates systematic from
sampling error on real networks, with a false-positive rate controlled by the calibrated null and
confirmed distribution-free, the sharpest use of the free calibration, and one an overconfident
learned $\sigma$ destroys.

\subsection{What survives a second replicate}\label{sec:stability}
The benchmark ships three independent repeats, so the flag set can be recomputed rather than
assumed. It does not hold up as a set. The same pipeline at the same FDR level returns
$\{$\texttt{bace}, \texttt{brd4}, \texttt{cdk8}, \texttt{faah}, \texttt{hif2a}, \texttt{p38}$\}$ on
the first replicate, $\{$\texttt{bace}, \texttt{brd4}, \texttt{ciordia\_retro}, \texttt{hif2a},
\texttt{p38}, \texttt{renin}$\}$ on the second and $\{$\texttt{bace}, \texttt{cdk8},
\texttt{renin}$\}$ on the third: eight systems are flagged at least once, one
(\texttt{bace}) is flagged every time, and pairwise Jaccard overlap is $0.50$, $0.29$, $0.29$
against $0.072$ for a random draw of the same size. The churn is not a threshold artefact and
persists from $\alpha=0.01$ to $\alpha=0.20$ (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:Stab}). Pooling the
three replicates flags seven systems, the union minus \texttt{faah}.

Two things do survive. The ranking of systems by closure inconsistency reproduces across
replicates (Spearman $0.649$, $0.644$, $0.723$ over all $48$ systems), and the dependence of
selectivity on $\sigma$-calibration, which is the claim this section is actually making, is
essentially unmoved: the $\times0.15$ shrink flags $42$, $41$, $41$ systems, the calibrated bar
$6$, $6$, $3$, a $\times1.3$ inflation $1$, $1$, $1$, and a $\times2$ inflation none, on the three
replicates respectively. What a single replicate supports is a ranking and a calibrated operating
point, not a list of names. Among the published six, \texttt{faah} is the weakest on every
measure: its reduced $\chi^2$ crosses one in both directions across replicates
($3.71$, $0.45$, $1.47$), its standardized residuals barely reproduce between replicates
($r=-0.085$), and it is the one member the pooled fit drops.

\subsection{Out-of-sample behaviour}\label{sec:oos}
Two checks use one replicate to predict another and so cannot be circular. First, the per-edge
miscalibration measured from replicates two and three predicts the closure statistic of the
replicate they exclude: writing $V_e^{\text{rep}}=c_e^2V_e$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cons} makes
$\mathbb{E}[\chi^2_\nu]$ the curl-leverage-weighted mean of $c_e^{-2}$, and that prediction
correlates with the observed per-system $\chi^2_\nu$ at Spearman $0.648$
($p=6\times10^{-7}$, $n=48$; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:OOS}A). The aggregate level also
matches, $\mathbb{E}[\chi^2_\nu]=0.85$ with a system-cluster bootstrap interval $[0.63,1.08]$,
a bar-width factor of $1.09\times$ $[0.96,1.26]$. This is a quantitative point prediction of a
per-system statistic from data that do not enter it. The flagged systems sit \emph{above} the
line, which is what reproducible systematic error should do: it inflates a closure residual
without inflating the spread between repeats.

Second, edges selected on one replicate are more inconsistent on the two held out than
equal-size random selections are (mean gain in held-out reduced $\chi^2$, Stouffer
$p=1.3\times10^{-3}$, $1.3\times10^{-3}$, $4.4\times10^{-3}$ over the three rotations), while a
topology-only control built from curl-leverage is indistinguishable from random
($p=0.21$--$0.91$). We calibrated each candidate statistic against a no-error null before using
it and report the realized level of all of them, including the two we discarded for firing far
above nominal. Only the consistency-gain statistic is specific to the flagged systems: an
$|z|$-rank-matched control drawn from unflagged systems reproduces the leverage-normalised
residual signal but not the consistency gain. The effect is an aggregate one, significant
individually in two of the five testable systems.

\section{Chirality completeness}\label{sec:chiral}
A geometric readout of a ligand, whether the $E(3)$-equivariant networks that are standard for
this task~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} or the descriptor-based
predictor used here (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), has to distinguish stereoisomers. Which readout
channels does that require? The statement below is a property of the readout's symmetry group, so
it binds any such model, including the one this work uses.
\begin{theorem}[Chirality completeness]\label{thm:chiral}
Any $O(3)$-invariant readout (a function of pairwise distances, norms, or dot products) is
constant on an enantiomer pair $\{M,M^\ast\}$ ($M^\ast$ the mirror image) and is therefore
enantiomer-blind, so \emph{some} parity-odd channel is necessary. The pseudoscalar
$\chi(M)=\det[\mathbf r_2{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_3{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_4{-}\mathbf r_1]$
(the $\texttt{0o}$ triple product of four readout points $\mathbf r_i$) satisfies
$\chi(AM)=\det(A)\chi(M)$ for $A\in O(3)$, so it is $SO(3)$-invariant and flips sign under
reflection; $(\{\|\mathbf r_i{-}\mathbf r_j\|\},\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\chi)$ is a complete $SO(3)$
invariant (complete for distinguishing the enantiomer pair $\{M,\sigma M\}$ once the full distance set fixes the cloud up to $O(3)$; fully characterizing the chirality of a many-atom molecule needs a consistent set of pseudoscalars),
\textbf{provided the four readout points $\mathbf r_1,\ldots,\mathbf r_4$ are non-coplanar}: $\chi$
is the (signed) volume of the parallelepiped they span (six times the tetrahedron volume), so it
vanishes identically whenever the points are coplanar (the three difference vectors become
linearly dependent), and a vanishing
$\chi$ carries no sign, hence no chirality bit, regardless of the underlying molecule's true
handedness. Thus, for a non-coplanar readout quadruple, a single
$\texttt{0o}$ triple-product channel is a \emph{sufficient} minimal addition that restores the
chirality bit; the trunk's readout must select (or the architecture must guarantee) such a
non-degenerate quadruple for the completeness statement to apply.
\end{theorem}
Consistent with the paper's principle, the chirality bit is \emph{read off} the geometry (a
pseudoscalar), not learned; this makes precise a fact familiar from the equivariant-network
literature (the role of odd irreps), stated here as an architectural-completeness requirement on
the trunk that feeds the bottleneck rather than as a central result. Supplementary
Figure~\ref{fig:E} realizes it: an even readout gives bit-identical outputs on every
enantiomer pair (collapse $=0$ to machine precision), so its best possible chiral $\ddG$
prediction is zero; adding the $\texttt{0o}$ channel cuts enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE $11.7\times$
($1.67\to0.14$ kcal/mol).

\section{An applicability map: where this calibration helps downstream, and where it does not}\label{sec:audit}
Having established the primitive's real-data payoff (the cycle-closure detector of
Section~\ref{sec:qc}), this section delimits the downstream tasks it does \emph{not} improve.
This is an applicability map that scopes the free calibration to decisions and detection, not a
catalogue of failures. The free, differentiable $\sigma$ is attractive; whether it improves
downstream machine learning is a separate question. We test it on four tasks (interval sharpness,
edge ranking, commit-to-synthesis selection, and reward re-ranking over a measured congeneric set,
single target \texttt{eg5}) on the public OpenFF
protein--ligand benchmark~\citep{hahn2022plbench}, which aggregates the FEP+ and Merck data
sets~\citep{wang2015fep,schindler2020merck} (experimental $\ddG$ labels; both-endpoint
scaffold-disjoint out-of-distribution (OOD) splits), and in controlled simulations. The
amortized $\sigma$ we test (the decomposed physics $\sigma$, written
$\sigma_{\text{total}}$) is a deep-ensemble trunk's
$\sigma_{\text{total}}=q\sqrt{\sigma^2_{\text{ens}}+\sigma^2_{\text{ale}}}$: an epistemic
ensemble spread $\sigma_{\text{ens}}$ and a benchmark aleatoric floor $\sigma_{\text{ale}}$,
conformally scaled by $q$ at miscoverage $\alpha=0.1$ (90\% target coverage). This
$\sigma_{\text{total}}$ is a different quantity from Figure~\ref{fig:A}'s per-edge BAR
sampling-se and its learned-variance foil: Figure~\ref{fig:A} asks whether a model can learn to
\emph{predict} the sampling variance of a single BAR $\dFh$ from its own work statistics (found
overconfident), whereas $\sigma_{\text{total}}$ here is the surrogate's downstream
\emph{predictive} uncertainty on unseen $\ddG$ values (ensemble epistemic spread plus an
aleatoric floor), tested on the OpenFF benchmark rather than the controlled work-sampling model of
Figure~\ref{fig:A}; the two are evaluated on different data and answer different questions. Any
bare ``$\sigma$'' below this point refers to $\sigma_{\text{total}}$ unless a figure reference
says otherwise.

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:audit} collects the effect of this decomposed $\sigma$, relative to
the relevant baseline, across all six downstream tasks audited below: no measurable gain on
interval sharpness, edge weighting, or commit ranking; a measured loss on reward re-ranking over
the single evaluable congeneric target (\texttt{eg5}); and a clear benefit on commit trust and on
the stopping decision.

\paragraph{Trust: an overconfident $\sigma$ corrupts decisions; this decomposed $\sigma$ does not, and
calibration is cheap (on the OpenFF benchmark).} The clearest payoff of calibration is a trustworthy stop/commit
decision. Figure~\ref{fig:J}B isolates the effect of $\sigma$ by holding the (ensemble) mean
fixed and varying only the uncertainty used to commit a candidate to synthesis (commit iff the
lower-confidence bound (LCB) $\mu-\kappa\sigma\ge\tau$, with $\kappa=z_{1-\alpha}$ the
confidence multiplier). An overconfident epistemic-only
$\sigma$ over-claims badly: its realized-versus-claimed correctness shortfall is $+0.182$
(CI $[0.132,0.225]$) larger than the decomposed $\sigma$'s, whereas the decomposed physics
$\sigma$ is calibrated-to-conservative. This trust is \emph{not unique} to
the physics $\sigma$: applying standard temperature scaling~\citep{guo2017calibration} or
split-conformal recalibration to the same overconfident $\sigma$ recovers the trustworthiness
(temperature$-$ours $+0.034$, CI $[-0.018,0.081]$; conformal$-$ours $-0.029$, CI
$[-0.077,0.007]$; both indistinguishable from ours). Trustworthy
decisions therefore require \emph{some} proper calibration, which the physics $\sigma$ supplies at no
calibration-set cost for the aleatoric term and, uniquely, as a differentiable graph weight.
Even a \emph{conservative} bar carries a decision cost: a systematic
${\sim}1.41\times$ se inflation is itself a (safe-direction) miscalibration that, via
the LCB rule $\mu-\kappa\sigma\ge\tau$, biases toward late stopping and under-commit. It errs
safe against sampling noise; and because the independent-replicate SD is itself a lower bound
on full reproducibility (Section~\ref{sec:bar}, Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}), the inflation may be
closer to neutral against the true run-to-run spread once pose and system-preparation variance
are included.

\paragraph{Sharpness: this decomposed $\sigma$ is not sharper on real out-of-distribution residuals (OpenFF benchmark).} A natural hope is
that a decomposed, adaptive $\sigma$ yields tighter intervals than a flat conformal band at
equal coverage. On a controlled 1-D problem whose generative structure the decomposition knows,
it does (we keep that illustration in the supplement). On \emph{real} held-out trunk residuals
(Figure~\ref{fig:Breal}), at matched $\sim$0.90 marginal coverage, it does \emph{not}: the
decomposed interval is slightly \emph{wider} than flat split-conformal (width ratio
split/ours $0.92\times$), because the epistemic spread is small relative to the large
irreducible OOD error (correlation of half-width with $|$residual$|$ only $+0.11$). Its
conditional calibration is marginally better than a flat band and comparable to a fair
Mondrian-by-target conformal (per-target max coverage error $0.17$ vs $0.22$ for both split
and Mondrian), but the advantage is not significant
(ours$-$split $-0.044$, CI $[-0.121,0.005]$). The synthetic result (up to $2.2\times$ sharper
than split-conformal, $2.0\times$ sharper than CQR) does not transfer to real data.

\paragraph{Ranking and selection: the choice of $\sigma$ is second-order at matched budget.}
For edge ranking in active learning, sandwich versus naive edge weights differ by
$0.98\times$ $[0.93,1.03]$ on the primary calls-to-top-$k$ metric (24-seed bootstrap CI;
Figure~\ref{fig:C}, supplement): in a connected measurement graph the GLS contrast mean is
unbiased for \emph{any} positive weights (Gauss--Markov), so the weight
choice is second-order
for ranking. The same holds for commit selection: at matched commit \emph{volume}
(Figure~\ref{fig:J}A, ranking candidates by the standardized margin $(\mu-\tau)/\sigma$ and
committing the top fraction), the $\sigma$-aware ranking does not beat a raw-$\mu$ ranking
(precision at a 25\% budget $0.674$ vs $0.680$; trunk$-$raw $-0.006$, CI $[-0.016,0.007]$). A
calibrated bar says \emph{whether} to act; it does not change \emph{which} action ranks
first.

\paragraph{Reward re-ranking (evaluable congeneric set): this decomposed $\sigma$ does not help, and where $\sigma$ varies it hurts.} We drive
a trajectory-balance GFlowNet~\citep{bengio2021gflownet,malkin2022tb} over an evaluable ligand
space (a held-out target's measured congeneric series; trunk trained target-disjoint) with three
rewards: the calibrated LCB, the raw predicted value, and an overconfident
MVE reward. On \texttt{eg5}, the on-disk target with the \emph{largest} $\sigma$ spread (so the
calibrated reward genuinely re-ranks candidates), the calibrated reward \emph{lowers} the true
hit-rate of the sampled distribution ($0.140$ vs raw $0.250$ and MVE $0.256$; cal$-$raw
$-0.110$, CI $[-0.151,-0.073]$; Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:K}). The mechanism is a positive
correlation between uncertainty and affinity,
$\mathrm{corr}(\sigma_{\text{total}},\text{affinity})=+0.42$: the high-$\sigma$ ligands are
the chemically novel \emph{strong} binders, so the LCB penalty $-\kappa\sigma$ demotes the true
winners. Calibration would help reward re-ranking only if $\sigma$ flagged \emph{low-quality}
candidates; on congeneric chemistry it flags \emph{novelty} instead. This is the best case
available for calibrated reward re-ranking (the most $\sigma$-varying target available), and it
fails. From this single target we draw a \emph{hypothesis}, not a transferable diagnostic: an LCB reward may
help only when $\sigma$ flags \emph{low quality}; whenever $\sigma$ instead flags \emph{novelty},
as it does on this congeneric series, the bound penalizes exactly the candidates worth making,
and an uncalibrated reward is the safer default. Because this rests on one evaluable target, we
state it as a hypothesis to be tested rather than a general rule: a regime where $\sigma$ tracks
quality (e.g.\ diverse libraries with genuine actives and decoys) is where calibrated re-ranking
should pay, and is the
natural next test.

\paragraph{Where it pays: the stopping decision.} The one place a calibrated bar clearly pays is
knowing when to \emph{stop} an active-learning loop. Figure~\ref{fig:G} runs two learners with
the same correct (true-precision GLS) posterior mean but different assumed uncertainty (a
distinct, simulated posterior variance, not $\sigma_{\text{total}}$ above): the calibrated
learner's claimed confidence tracks actual top-$k$ correctness ($|\Delta|=0.022$) and stops at
$30$ calls $82\%$ correct, while an overconfident learner (its assumed posterior se scaled by
$0.15\times$, the ${\sim}7\times$ central overconfidence \emph{measured} by Figure~\ref{fig:A}'s
sampling-se foil and reused here only as a numeric shrink factor) stops twice as early at
only $60\%$ correct. This is a controlled simulation,
but it is the same lesson as the commit panel: calibration is a trust instrument.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} Five independent tests, namely sharpness, edge weighting, commit ranking,
reward re-ranking over a measured congeneric set (single target \texttt{eg5}), and (positively) stopping, agree. A calibrated per-edge
$\sigma$ does not sharpen intervals on real data, does not re-rank what to compute or commit,
and does not steer reward re-ranking toward better molecules on this evaluable congeneric set; an \emph{overconfident} $\sigma$,
however, silently corrupts the stop/commit decision. Calibration is therefore a
\emph{trust/decision instrument}, not a performance multiplier, and the physics bottleneck
delivers that calibration for free and differentiably. The differentiable graph weight earns its
place through \emph{structure}, not magnitude: it makes the gauge directions exactly
unidentifiable (knowledge gradient $=0$) and supplies the trust calculation, even though, by the
same Gauss--Markov argument that makes weighting second-order, the numerical \emph{value} of the
weight does not improve ranking. The value of reading the variance off the physics is therefore a
calibrated decision and an identifiable acquisition (and the quality-control detector
of Section~\ref{sec:qc}), not a sharper or better-ranked prediction.

\section{Computational details}\label{sec:methods}
\paragraph{Data.} The controlled panel of Figure~\ref{fig:A} uses the \texttt{alchemtest} BACE1
relative-binding and benzene sets, from which forward and reverse work samples are available per
$\lambda$ window. The replicate validation, the cycle-closure test and every experiment on real
binding data use the OpenFE IndustryBenchmarks2024 release~\citep{baumann2025industrybenchmarks},
which ships three independent protocol repeats per transformation: $1145$ edges over $34$ targets,
of which $48$ systems contain at least one cycle and therefore admit the closure test. Grounding
against measured affinity uses public ChEMBL $\mathrm{IC}_{50}$ values. No free-energy simulation
was run for this work; all alchemical output is taken as published.

\paragraph{Per-edge variance.} $\dFh$ is obtained by solving Bennett's estimating equation
$S(\Delta F)=0$ by scalar root-find, and its variance from the closed form $B/I^2$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sand} evaluated at the root. The backward pass uses the implicit function
theorem, $\partial\dFh/\partial\theta=(1/I)\,\partial_\theta S$, so it costs one division and does
not unroll the root-find; gradients are checked against finite differences to machine precision.

\paragraph{Cycle-closure test.} Node potentials are fitted by generalized least squares on the
signed incidence matrix of the perturbation network, whitened by the per-edge standard errors, so
that the fit is a weighted least-squares problem with conductances $1/V_e$. The statistic is the
reduced $\chi^2$ of the residuals with $\mathrm{dof}=E-\operatorname{rank}(B)$; per-system tail
probabilities use the exact regularized upper incomplete gamma function rather than a
Wilson--Hilferty approximation, and systems are flagged by Benjamini--Hochberg control of the
false discovery rate at $\alpha=0.05$ across systems.

\paragraph{Amortized predictor.} The predictor used in the acquisition, stopping and reward
experiments is deliberately small, and it is not an equivariant network. A ligand is featurized as
a $1024$-bit ECFP4 Morgan fingerprint (radius $2$, chirality flag off) concatenated with six
achiral RDKit descriptors: molecular weight, Crippen $\log P$, topological polar surface area, and
counts of hydrogen-bond donors, hydrogen-bond acceptors and rotatable bonds. An edge is
represented by the difference of its two endpoint feature vectors. The parity-odd channel of
Section~\ref{sec:chiral} is a single additional coordinate, the difference of a summed signed
volume over the assigned stereocentres of an ETKDGv3 embedding, giving $1031$ features per edge;
every other coordinate is invariant under reflection, so this coordinate is the only carrier of
chirality. The regressor is a bootstrap deep ensemble of eight multilayer perceptrons, each with
two hidden layers of $64$ units and SiLU activations and a scalar output, trained for $300$ epochs
on a squared-error objective with Adam at learning rate $5\times10^{-3}$ and weight decay
$10^{-4}$, on inputs standardized by the training mean and standard deviation. Each member is
seeded independently and fitted to its own bootstrap resample; the epistemic term is the spread
across members, and the total $\sigma$ combines it in quadrature with the aleatoric floor before a
conformal rescaling.

\paragraph{Software.} Python $\geq3.11$ with numpy $2.5.0$, scipy $1.18.0$, pymbar $4.0.3$, torch
$2.12.1$, RDKit $2026.3.3$ and matplotlib $3.11.0$. Ensemble training runs on CPU; torch reductions
on CPU are not bitwise reproducible across builds, so ensemble-dependent figures can move in the
third decimal between machines while every verdict reported here is unchanged.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
The sandwich is aleatoric-only and conditional on correctly sampled ensembles; systematic
force-field bias is outside it, and the out-of-distribution behavior we audit rests on an
ensemble plus a scalar aleatoric floor (there is no learned force-field-correction head, so the
three-way decomposition of the synthetic illustration is not reproduced on real data). The
binding-edge se is validated against the three independent replicates of the OpenFE
IndustryBenchmarks2024 (Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}): in aggregate it is conservative by
$\sim$$1.4\times$, though calibration is
heterogeneous per system ($28$ of $34$ targets conservative; one protonation-variant outlier
overconfident). The three replicates are seed/velocity-only repeats from identical starting
coordinates (OpenFE \texttt{protocol\_repeats}), so the across-replicate SD reflects
sampling/convergence noise and is a lower bound on full reproducibility (excluding pose and
system-preparation variance); the $\sim$$1.41\times$ conservatism may therefore be
closer to neutral against real reproducibility. The
gauge-acquisition and stopping experiments are controlled simulations. The reward re-ranking audit is a
single $\sigma$-varying target (\texttt{eg5}); the conclusion that $\sigma$ tracks novelty rather than quality
should be tested across more chemotypes. Point estimates also shift modestly run-to-run (torch CPU non-determinism), so the single-target re-ranking result is directional. The cycle-closure quality-control test (Section~\ref{sec:qc}) detects only \emph{edge-level} inconsistency (node-consistent force-field bias cancels around a loop and is invisible to it), and its flags are validated retrospectively against held-out replicates and against experiment both on one clean system (\texttt{eg5}, where acting on the flags neither helps nor harms) and, now, on four flagged systems grounded against public ChEMBL affinity (\texttt{cdk8, hif2a, p38, bace}), where guided QC-edge removal ties random removal on MUE-vs-experiment (combined Stouffer $p=0.48$; Figure~\ref{fig:Lcausal}), confirming the same scope boundary on real, flagged, multi-system data: the QC repairs internal consistency, not force-field-limited absolute accuracy. This retrospective test is powered only by $K=1$--$3$ guided removals per system and by a connectivity-based ChEMBL join (stereo-blind, so enantiomer pairs sharing a 2D graph collapse to one experimental value, inflating absolute MUE but canceling in the guided-vs-random contrast since both arms read the identical cached affinity); a real but small effect could be underpowered at this $n=4$. The fully prospective flag$\to$repair$\to$remeasure test on a flagged system, with new MD rather than a retrospective affinity join, remains future work. The distribution-free cross-replicate check establishes that what is flagged reproduces across independent runs, but it is not a quantitative false-positive-rate guarantee: it does not certify the nominal Benjamini--Hochberg rate itself, only that what is flagged reproduces. The detector's \emph{recall} (false-negative rate) is likewise uncharacterized (there are no ground-truth error labels), and the median reduced $\chi^2=0.34$ (Section~\ref{sec:qc}) implies limited single-replicate power, so the six flagged systems are a \emph{lower bound} on the systems carrying systematic error, not a prevalence estimate: on the same replicate data, \texttt{renin} is systematic by the pooled-replicate ratio (reduced $\chi^2$ $2.5\to4.7$ pooling the three replicates, the same pooled-ratio signature Panel~C uses to confirm the six flagged systems; ratio $1.9$, between the sampling-only pole at $1$ and the systematic pole at $3$) yet did not clear FDR on a single replicate, a documented systematic-but-unflagged case (\texttt{docs/results\_figL.md}). Finally, a structure-based (docking) reverse-screen we
built as a follow-on did not beat a ligand-similarity baseline on this benchmark, so the
structure-directed regime is left to future work rather than claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper reports a relative binding free energy uncertainty that is computed rather than
learned: the Bennett Acceptance Ratio estimator's own finite-sampling variance, in closed
form, with a backward pass that costs a single division rather than an unrolled computation. The
same variance becomes a conductance on the network of alchemical measurements, giving an
acquisition weight that is exactly blind to gauge-redundant directions, and the predictor that
feeds it carries a parity-odd readout channel, without which the readout cannot tell a molecule
from its mirror image.

Validated against independent simulation replicates on real protein-ligand binding data, this
calibrated error bar turns thermodynamic cycle closure into a quality-control test that
separates systematic simulation error from ordinary sampling noise, and an overconfident learned
error bar destroys that same test. Audited across further downstream uses, sharper
prediction intervals, better edge ranking, better synthesis-commit decisions, and better
reward-guided re-ranking, the calibrated uncertainty delivers none of them; what it
delivers, beyond detection, is a trustworthy commit decision and a trustworthy stopping rule for
an active-learning loop. Reading uncertainty off the physics is therefore a decision and
quality-control instrument, earned for
free from an estimator the field already runs, rather than a route to sharper or better-ranked
predictions.
