### 2. Cycle-closure QC novelty vs DiffNet / arsenic-cinnabar, and is "controlled FPR" real?
**Ask** — Weighted-network MLE (xu2019diffnet) and hysteresis QC (wang2015fep) already produce weighted residuals and flag bad edges. What is new beyond wrapping a weighted residual in a chi-square p-value? Is the "controlled FPR" a demonstrated property given non-normal, small-sample, autocorrelated per-edge errors, or a nominal label? And does the 6-system flag list survive if you use raw pymbar se instead of the sandwich (they coincide to leading order — so Panel B may be calibrated-vs-deliberately-miscalibrated, a strawman)?
**Position** — Defensible as-is; the fix is positioning, not new results.
**Drafted response** — The novelty is specific and we will state it more sharply against DiffNet/cinnabar rather than only cite them. Standard practice fits the weighted network and inspects residuals or fixed hysteresis cutoffs; what it lacks is a *validated per-edge null* that makes the goodness-of-fit a calibrated test with per-edge-adaptive thresholds (`paper_body.tex:471-475`). Three genuinely new pieces: (i) the per-edge variance V_e = B_e/I_e² is replicate-validated (Fig A-rep, 1.25–1.41× conservative, not overconfident), so the reference distribution is anchored to measured reproducibility, which ad-hoc cutoffs are not; (ii) Panel B is not calibrated-vs-strawman — the honest foil is the community's increasingly popular *learned* σ (×0.15, flags 88%, FPR→1) vs the physics σ (flags 12%, selective); raw pymbar se equals the sandwich to leading order and gives essentially the same 12%, which is our own coincidence claim, not a hidden weakness; (iii) the replicate-based systematic-vs-sampling separator (Panel C: pooling drives flagged χ² toward the 3× variance-components ceiling, which only reproducible error can do) is new. On chi-square validity: we do not lean on asymptotic normality alone — the causal-repair test (guided removal restores closure in 3–7 edges vs 12–37 random) and cross-replicate residual correlation (r = +0.30 to +0.42, n=1143) are distribution-free confirmations (`paper_body.tex:507-513`). We add a paragraph explicitly contrasting our calibrated null with DiffNet's MLE and cinnabar's residuals, and soften "controlled FPR" to "FPR controlled to the extent the per-edge null is calibrated (validated in Fig A-rep), with distribution-free causal/replicate confirmation."
**If pushed** — Add an SI robustness check that the 6-system flag list is identical under raw-pymbar-se weights vs sandwich weights (expected: identical, reinforcing "they coincide"). Cost: low (one script over existing `src/bar/qc.py`). No new conclusions.

#### S1. Wilson–Hilferty χ² p-values are materially inaccurate at the low per-cycle dof actually tested
**Ask** — The χ² tail p-values driving the BH-FDR flags use the Wilson–Hilferty cube-root approximation (`src/bar/qc.py:86`), but several flagged systems have very small dof (`brd4` dof=1, `faah` dof=8, `bace` dof=14). WH is a large-dof asymptotic, unreliable in the far upper tail at dof=1–few; are the reported p-values (down to ~1.9e-10) trustworthy, and could the flag set be an approximation artifact?
**Position** — cheap-fix (concern is real and quantitatively confirmed, but it changes no conclusion).
**Drafted response** — The referee is correct that WH is inaccurate here, and we now say so and fix it. Against the exact χ² survival function (`scipy.stats.chi2.sf`), WH is **conservative** in the extreme tail — it over-states p by 64% at `brd4` (dof=1, X²=15.4: WH 1.43e-4 vs exact 8.7e-5) and by ~190% at `bace` (dof=14, X²=78: WH 1.9e-10 vs exact 6.7e-11). Because the exact p is always *smaller* than the reported WH p, the six flags can only strengthen: re-running BH-FDR at α=0.05 with exact p returns the identical set {bace, brd4, cdk8, faah, hif2a, p38} (verified). The one-line fix replaces `chi2_sf` with the exact regularized-gamma upper tail (`scipy.special.gammaincc(k/2, x/2)` or `scipy.stats.chi2.sf`); scipy is already a core package dependency. The reduced-χ² point estimates and the median 0.34 are unaffected (they never use WH).
**If pushed** — Swap `chi2_sf` for the exact tail, regenerate the Fig L p-column/caption, and add an exact-vs-WH regression check to `tests/test_qc.py`. Cost: low (< 1 h, no new data, one deterministic figure rebuild).

#### S2. χ² validity under small, non-Gaussian per-edge errors — and dof=1 "systems" that are single hypotheses
**Ask** — The goodness-of-fit test assumes each edge residual is Gaussian with known variance V_e, but per-edge FEP errors are neither exactly Gaussian nor have known variance (V_e is estimated). 9 of 48 systems have exactly one independent cycle (dof=1) and 23 have dof≤3, so "system flagged" often rests on one or a handful of residuals; brd4 (8 edges, 1 cycle) is flagged on essentially one closure number. Is the nominal χ² reference valid, and is per-system flagging at dof=1 honest?
**Position** — defensible-as-is, with one scope sentence to add.
**Drafted response** — We agree the χ² reference is an approximation and deliberately do not rest the claim on it alone. (i) A model-free backstop supplies robustness: Panel C's variance-components separator and the out-of-sample replicate-residual correlation (r = +0.30 to +0.42, n=1143; `results_figL.md`) assume no Gaussianity, and pure sampling noise would drive both to zero — the flags reproduce regardless of the tail model. (ii) The reduced-χ² interpretation is empirically calibrated: the median across 48 systems is 0.34, so the bars are ≥1.7× conservative and the threshold at χ²_ν=1 biases against false flags. (iii) On dof=1 we already state (`results_figL.md`, "Localization") that a single-cycle system such as brd4 is correctly flagged at the *system* level while its culprit edge is *not identifiable*; the flag is a legitimate single hypothesis (the loop closes or it doesn't) and survives the causal-repair and replicate-reproduction checks. We add one sentence making per-system dof=1 flagging explicit as a test of one closure constraint, not a localized diagnosis.
**If pushed** — For the flagged systems, replace the χ² p with a parametric-bootstrap or sign-flip permutation p as a non-asymptotic reference; medium cost (~3–4 h, no new MD), expected to corroborate the six flags.
