\paragraph{Autocorrelation robustness: raw $n$ vs $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n/g$ (Figure~\ref{fig:gsweep}).}
The sandwich $B=n_f\Var_f[p]+n_r\Var_r[p]$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sand}) assumes independent samples,
whereas real molecular-dynamics works are autocorrelated. On the $19$ real BACE1 RBFE binding edges
of Figure~\ref{fig:A}B (no new MD), we recompute the sandwich se at the raw sample count and again
after decorrelating each leg to its statistical inefficiency $g$~\citep{shirts2008mbar}
(geometric-mean $\bar g=1.48$, range $[1.15,2.16]$; median count $n{=}500\to349$). Against the
correlation-aware truth (the decorrelated bootstrap SD, the same reference as Figure~\ref{fig:A}B),
the $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ sandwich is calibrated (reported/truth $1.00$, $[0.93,1.07]$, reproducing
Figure~\ref{fig:A}B), while the raw-$n$ sandwich is overconfident by $0.83\times$ ($[0.67,0.97]$),
matching the $1/\sqrt{\bar g}=0.82$ autocorrelation prediction. The sandwich also tracks its
\emph{own} matched (raw) bootstrap ($1.00$, $[0.92,1.05]$): the closed form is internally
consistent, and the only quantity that must be right is the effective sample count, which the paper
decorrelates to $g$ throughout (Section~\ref{sec:bar}, paper text). The calibration verdict is
therefore robust to autocorrelation once $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is used, as it is in every real-data
panel. Deterministic; \texttt{make gsweep}.

\paragraph{Corrected learned-variance foils and the data-efficiency floor (Figure~\ref{fig:A}A).}
Corrected foils do not rescue the head at a realistic budget: a $\beta$-NLL
head~\citep{seitzer2022pitfalls} ($\beta{=}0.5$, the standard objective fix) does \emph{not}
recover calibration (se ${\approx}0.11\times$, in fact slightly worse than plain NLL's
$0.15\times$), and at this training seed a $5$-member deep
ensemble~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} reaches only the large-budget floor
(se ${\approx}0.20\times$, the same ${\approx}5\times$ overconfidence a $20\times$-larger-budget
plain head reaches).
The residual $\sim$5$\times$ overconfidence of the plain, oracle, and $\beta$-NLL heads is
therefore a
\emph{data-efficiency} limit of a per-edge \emph{learned} variance, not an identifiability barrier
and not specific to the Gaussian-NLL objective: the conditional variance $se(\text{overlap})$ is
itself identifiable from single labels, pooling single-label edges by overlap recovering the
sandwich to $\sim$6\% at $4000$ edges ($\sim$4\% at $40000$).

\paragraph{Seed stability of the learned-variance foils (Figure~\ref{fig:A}A).}
These ratios are stable across independent training seeds for the plain and
$\beta$-NLL heads: over $5$ seeds the plain head spans $0.1229$--$0.2103\times$ (mean
$0.1537\times$) and the $\beta$-NLL head $0.1024$--$0.1256\times$ (mean $0.1147\times$), so their
overconfidence is a property of the estimator at this budget rather than of one lucky
initialization. The $5$-member ensemble is not stable: across the same $5$ seeds, each with a
disjoint $5$-network member block, it spans $0.1984$--$1.2200\times$ (mean $0.4198\times$, sd
$0.4481\times$), and one seed \emph{overestimates} the true se by $22\%$, crossing perfect
calibration. Unlike the plain, oracle ($0.1941$--$0.2571\times$, mean $0.2101\times$), and
$\beta$-NLL heads, whose overconfidence is robust across independent retrains, the ensemble is
unreliable in \emph{both} directions at this budget. Deterministic; \texttt{make figAseeds}.

\paragraph{Structural context of the flagged systems (Figure~\ref{fig:qcstruct}).}
The six systems the cycle-closure QC flags (Figure~\ref{fig:L}) are the ones carrying an
independently-documented hard structural feature, read here straight from public co-crystal
structures. In BACE1 (PDB 4DJW) the catalytic aspartic-acid dyad hydrogen-bonds the inhibitor
(Asp93 and Asp289, both $2.7$\,\AA); the protonation state of an aspartic-protease dyad is the
textbook source of free-energy error. In BRD4 BD1 (PDB 3MXF, in complex with
JQ1~\citep{filippakopoulos2010bet}) the acetyl-lysine pocket packs $16$ crystallographic waters
within $4.5$\,\AA\ of the ligand ($8$ within $3.5$\,\AA) around the recognition asparagine Asn140
($3.1$\,\AA); this conserved water network is the known BRD4 free-energy challenge~\citep{aldeghi2016binding}.
The other flags carry analogous a-priori difficulties: \texttt{faah} (covalent serine hydrolase,
large acyl channel), \texttt{cdk8} and \texttt{p38} (large scaffold and R-group changes), and
\texttt{hif2a} (a buried polar cavity). This is retrospective structural \emph{context} for why
the flags are chemically sensible, not a per-edge causal claim: cycle closure is edge-level and
blind to node-consistent bias (main text), and the point is only that the flagged set coincides
with the features a domain expert would flag a priori. Deterministic; \texttt{make qcstruct}
(public PDBs cached in \texttt{data/pdb/}).

\paragraph{Per-edge observability map and predictive falsifier (Figure~\ref{fig:Lev}).} The
estimation--detection conservation law (Theorem~\ref{thm:cons}) certifies $\sum_e h_e=\mathrm{dof}$
to machine precision (maximum deviation $1.78\times10^{-14}$) across the $48$ cyclic systems and
identifies $48$ structurally un-auditable bridge edges ($h_e=0$, one per system on average); a
pre-registered test of whether the curl-leverage \emph{additionally} predicts the location of
reproducible systematic error, run on $934$ pooled edges over $45$ systems against the rule
confirmed iff $\rho>0$ and $p<0.05$ ($10^4$ within-system block permutations), is negative
(studentized Spearman $\rho=-0.073$, $p=0.9571$: KILL), so only the structural certificate is
claimed.

\paragraph{Fixed-cutoff head-to-head and $\chi^2$ reconciliation (Figure~\ref{fig:Cut}).} Two
pre-registered peer-review checks on the same $1145$ edges. P1: the calibrated per-edge null (AUC
$0.865$ scored against edges whose sign and magnitude reproduce across independent replicates)
does not measurably out-discriminate a
fixed $1.0$ kcal/mol hysteresis cutoff (AUC $0.575$) or a fixed-$\mathrm{se}$ $\chi^2$ test (AUC
$0.725$); paired bootstrap difference $+0.140$ $[-0.008,+0.316]$ (VERDICT: TIE). P2 tested whether
correlation among residuals of edges sharing a ligand endpoint explains the apparent gap between a
closure-implied se-conservatism ($1.71\times$, obtained as $1/\sqrt{0.34}$ from the median reduced
$\chi^2$) and the replicate-validated $1.41\times$. It does not (excess correlation $-0.0079$
$[-0.0314,+0.0164]$, VERDICT: NOT-CONFIRMED), and the Benjamini--Hochberg flag set is unchanged.
The pre-registered constants and this verdict are recorded here as run. We subsequently established
that the premise was unsound: by Theorem~\ref{thm:cons} the closure statistic identifies the
curl-leverage-weighted \emph{mean} of $c_e^{-2}$, not a median, so $1/\sqrt{\mathrm{median}}$ is not
an estimate of the same functional as the replicate ratio. There was accordingly no physical gap
for P2 to explain, and we do not revise the pre-registered constants after the fact. The calibrated null flags $6$ of $48$ systems, the
fixed-$\mathrm{se}$ $\chi^2$ test $11$, and the fixed cutoff $26$; the three flag sets are
strictly nested (calibrated $\subset$ fixed-$\mathrm{se}$ $\subset$ fixed cutoff), so on this
benchmark the calibrated null catches no system the fixed cutoff misses and instead removes
$20$ of its flags at a higher AUC. The $1.0$ kcal/mol cutoff was pre-registered at the
field-standard value before this comparison was run, and no sweep over alternative thresholds
was performed, so the comparison above is specific to that one fixed rule.

\paragraph{Per-system test statistics behind the flag sets (Tables~\ref{tab:stab}
and~\ref{tab:stab2}).} The flag sets summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:Stab} and in
Section~\ref{sec:stability} are thresholded Benjamini--Hochberg adjusted $p$-values, so the
underlying continuous quantities are reported in full rather than only their dichotomization.
For each system, a generalized-least-squares cycle-closure fit under the calibrated per-edge
null gives a $\chi^2$ on $\nu$ degrees of freedom, $\nu$ being the number of independent cycles
in that system's edge network; the reduced value $\chi^2_\nu=\chi^2/\nu$ and the raw upper-tail
probability follow, and those raw $p$-values are corrected for testing $48$ systems by the
Benjamini--Hochberg procedure at a false-discovery rate of $0.05$, giving the adjusted
$q$-values tabulated below. The $q\le0.05$ rule reproduces
\texttt{bar.qc.benjamini\_hochberg} exactly, which the generator asserts on every set it emits.
Each of the benchmark's three protocol replicates is analysed independently: the fit is rerun
from scratch on that replicate's edges and the correction is applied within that replicate
alone, so the three $q$ columns are not a joint error rate across replicates. All values are
transcribed from \texttt{docs/results\_figStab.md} (\texttt{make figStab}).

\paragraph{Dose-response of the QC to $\sigma$ miscalibration (Figure~\ref{fig:P4b}).}
Figure~\ref{fig:L}B and Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:P4} each characterize the cycle-closure test
at a small number of scale points (${\times}1$ calibrated, ${\times}0.15$ stress test / real
profile); here a pre-registered, frozen grid of $16$ uniform $\mathrm{se}$ scale factors
($2.0$--$0.10\times$) is swept through the identical GLS $+$ Benjamini--Hochberg pipeline on the
same $48$-system OpenFE network. Two frozen readouts: $s_{\text{onset}}$, the largest scale that
flags strictly more systems than calibrated, and $s_{50}$, the largest scale at which at least half
the systems are flagged. $s_{\text{onset}}=0.9\times$ ($7/48$ vs.\ the calibrated $6/48$) is exactly
one system crossing the Benjamini--Hochberg threshold. It is reported because it was
pre-registered, but it is not a measurement of this benchmark: because the flagged count is
monotone in the scale, a strictly-exceeds-calibrated onset readout is fully grid-determined,
returning whichever grid point below $1.0$ happens to sit just past the first system's threshold
crossing. Independent probing finds that system flipping as high as $s=0.995\times$, so a finer
grid would return $s_{\text{onset}}=0.995\times$, and in the continuum limit
$s_{\text{onset}}\to1.0\times$ for any benchmark with a system near the cutoff. A
magnitude-based readout, such as the largest scale that doubles the calibrated count
($0.7\times$ here, $12/48$), would have been the informative pre-registration. The substantive
change is mid-curve: from $0.7\times$ to $0.3\times$ the flagged fraction climbs from $12/48$
($25\%$) to $30/48$ ($62\%$), reaching half the benchmark at $s_{50}=0.3\times$ (the true $50\%$
crossing is bracketed by the adjacent $0.35\times$ and $0.30\times$ grid points, which flag
$23/48$ and $30/48$ respectively). The learned head's measured, rank-transferred miscalibration
band ($0.09$--$0.20\times$; Figure~\ref{fig:A}A, Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:P4}) lies entirely
below $s_{50}$, so the head is not near the transition but well inside the saturated regime. The
over-wide half of the grid is not flat either: relative to the calibrated $6/48$, a bar
only $30\%$ too wide ($1.3\times$) already loses five of the six flagged systems ($1/48$), and any
wider bar ($1.5\times$, $2.0\times$) flags none, so the requirement is calibration, not
conservatism. Two sanity
checks confirm the sweep reproduces the already-published test path: the ${\times}1.0$ row reads
$6/48$, matching Figure~\ref{fig:L}A's calibrated count, and the ${\times}0.15$ row reads $42/48$,
matching Figure~\ref{fig:L}B and Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:P4}'s uniform/real-profile arms.
Sweeping the shrink magnitude shows a graded rather than
threshold-like response, so the informative readout is where a majority is flagged: the flagged fraction climbs from the calibrated
$6/48$ ($12\%$) through a graded mid-curve rise, $12/48$ ($25\%$) at a $0.7\times$ scale to $30/48$
($62\%$) at $0.3\times$, reaching half the $48$-system benchmark at $s_{50}=0.3\times$, and the
learned head's measured miscalibration band ($0.09$--$0.20\times$) lies below that point, on the
deep-shrink side of where the test already flags a majority of systems. The quality-control test is
therefore selective over roughly a factor of three of $\sigma$ under-estimation below the
calibrated scale; over-wide $\sigma$ costs power in the other direction as well ($1.3\times$ flags
only $1/48$, and $1.5\times$ and $2.0\times$ flag none), so the calibrated scale is a two-sided
optimum rather than a plateau.

\paragraph{Per-system flag robustness to each system's own calibration error (Table~\ref{tab:P8}).}
The Figure~\ref{fig:L} flag set raises a mild circularity concern: the per-system calibration of
the sandwich null, measured independently against across-replicate spread
(Figure~\ref{fig:Arep}), is heterogeneous, and four of the six flagged systems are themselves
locally overconfident (\texttt{brd4} $0.76\times$, \texttt{faah} $0.90\times$, \texttt{bace}
$0.96\times$, \texttt{hif2a} $0.96\times$; only \texttt{cdk8} $1.41\times$ and \texttt{p38}
$1.43\times$ are conservative), so a flag could partly reflect that system's own too-tight bars
rather than genuine physical inconsistency. We test this directly: for replicate $0$, the $48$
systems with $\geq1$ independent cycle (the identical population as Figure~\ref{fig:L}), each
system's per-edge $\mathrm{se}$ is inflated by $1/\mathrm{ratio}$ where its own ratio is below
$1$ (systems with ratio $\geq1$ are already conservative and are left unchanged), and the
Benjamini--Hochberg test is recomputed over the whole set under this self-calibrated null.
Frozen pre-registration: SURVIVES iff every nominally-flagged system remains flagged;
PARTIAL/FAILS iff some/none do; the population, inflation rule, and $\alpha=0.05$ were fixed
before this run and not adjusted afterward. Result: \textbf{SURVIVES}. All six flags remain
under their own self-calibrated null (Table~\ref{tab:P8}), each self-calibrated reduced $\chi^2$
($2.36$--$8.97$) remaining far above the level a sampling-consistent network of this size and
degree-of-freedom profile shows (Section~\ref{sec:qc}, main text). The distribution-free
cross-replicate reproduction check of Figure~\ref{fig:Lval}B is a function of raw residual
correlation only, never of $\mathrm{se}$, so it does not depend on this null and is not affected
by this concern in either direction. The per-system ratio in Table~\ref{tab:P8} is the
root-mean-square reported standard error over the root-mean-square across-independent-replicate
SD, pooled per system, not a naive per-system average of per-edge ratios or a naive ratio of
arithmetic means, which for three of the six systems below would sit on the opposite side of
$\mathrm{ratio}=1$. Deterministic; \texttt{make figP8}; full $48$-system table in
\texttt{docs/results\_figP8.md}.

\paragraph{The decision cost of a conservative bar (Figure~\ref{fig:J}B).}
Even a \emph{conservative} bar carries a decision cost: a systematic
${\sim}1.41\times$ se inflation is itself a (safe-direction) miscalibration that, via
the LCB rule, biases toward late stopping and under-commit. It errs
safe against sampling noise; and because the independent-replicate SD is itself a lower bound
on full reproducibility (Section~\ref{sec:bar}), the inflation may be
closer to neutral against the true run-to-run spread once pose and system-preparation variance
are included.

\section{Chirality completeness}\label{sec:chiral}
A geometric readout of a ligand, whether the $E(3)$-equivariant networks that are standard for
this task~\citep{geiger2022e3nn,batzner2022nequip,batatia2022mace} or the descriptor-based
predictor used here (Section~\ref{sec:methods}), has to distinguish stereoisomers. Which readout
channels does that require? The statement below is a property of the readout's symmetry group, so
it binds any such model, including the one this work uses.
\begin{theorem}[Chirality completeness]\label{thm:chiral}
Any $O(3)$-invariant readout (a function of pairwise distances, norms, or dot products) is
constant on an enantiomer pair $\{M,M^\ast\}$ ($M^\ast$ the mirror image) and is therefore
enantiomer-blind, so \emph{some} parity-odd channel is necessary. The pseudoscalar
$\chi(M)=\det[\mathbf r_2{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_3{-}\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_4{-}\mathbf r_1]$
(the $\texttt{0o}$ triple product of four readout points $\mathbf r_i$) satisfies
$\chi(AM)=\det(A)\chi(M)$ for $A\in O(3)$, so it is $SO(3)$-invariant and flips sign under
reflection; $(\{\|\mathbf r_i{-}\mathbf r_j\|\},\,\mathrm{sgn}\,\chi)$ is a complete $SO(3)$
invariant (complete for distinguishing the enantiomer pair $\{M,\sigma M\}$ once the full distance set fixes the cloud up to $O(3)$; fully characterizing the chirality of a many-atom molecule needs a consistent set of pseudoscalars),
\textbf{provided the four readout points $\mathbf r_1,\ldots,\mathbf r_4$ are non-coplanar}: $\chi$
is the (signed) volume of the parallelepiped they span (six times the tetrahedron volume), so it
vanishes identically whenever the points are coplanar (the three difference vectors become
linearly dependent), and a vanishing
$\chi$ carries no sign, hence no chirality bit, regardless of the underlying molecule's true
handedness. Thus, for a non-coplanar readout quadruple, a single
$\texttt{0o}$ triple-product channel is a \emph{sufficient} minimal addition that restores the
chirality bit; the trunk's readout must select (or the architecture must guarantee) such a
non-degenerate quadruple for the completeness statement to apply.
\end{theorem}
Consistent with the paper's principle, the chirality bit is \emph{read off} the geometry (a
pseudoscalar), not learned; this makes precise a fact familiar from the equivariant-network
literature (the role of odd irreps), stated here as an architectural-completeness requirement on
the trunk that feeds the bottleneck rather than as a central result. Supplementary
Figure~\ref{fig:E} realizes it: an even readout gives bit-identical outputs on every
enantiomer pair (collapse $=0$ to machine precision), so its best possible chiral $\ddG$
prediction is zero; adding the $\texttt{0o}$ channel cuts enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE $11.7\times$
($1.67\to0.14$ kcal/mol).

\paragraph{Chirality completeness on the designed set (Figure~\ref{fig:E}).} An even
$O(3)$-invariant readout collapses every enantiomer pair exactly (collapse $=0$ to machine
precision, so its best possible chiral $\ddG$ prediction is zero); adding one parity-odd
$\texttt{0o}$ triple-product channel cuts enantiomer $\ddG$ MAE $11.7\times$ ($1.67\to0.14$
kcal/mol), realizing Theorem~\ref{thm:chiral}.

\paragraph{Chirality completeness on a real molecule (Figure~\ref{fig:Ereal}).}
Theorem~\ref{thm:chiral} is instantiated on thalidomide, the textbook case where the two
enantiomers differ in biological activity. We embed one enantiomer in 3D (RDKit) and reflect it
to the exact mirror geometry. Every O(3)-invariant readout is identical on the pair: all $171$
pairwise distances and all pairwise dot products (the Gram matrix) match to machine precision
($\max|\Delta|=0$), so a norm-and-dot-product readout is enantiomer-blind. The parity-odd
\texttt{0o} pseudoscalar $\chi=\det[p_1{-}p_0,p_2{-}p_0,p_3{-}p_0]$ flips sign
($+1.037\to-1.037$), and RDKit's CIP assignment flips $R\to S$. This reproduces, on real
chemistry, the collapse-and-restore of the chirality bit proved for the designed test set
(Figure~\ref{fig:E}). Deterministic; \texttt{make figEreal}.
